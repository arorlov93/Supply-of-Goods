# Prices, Tariffs and Landed Cost — Turkish Natural Stone and Ceramic Tile into the United States (research date: 26 September 2026)

> **Reader's warning.** Tariff policy affecting Turkish-origin goods changed at least three times between February and July 2026. Everything in Part B marked CONFIRMED was pulled from a primary source (USITC HTS database, Federal Register, USTR, CBP) on 26 September 2026. Everything marked REPORTED comes from trade press or law-firm summaries and must be re-verified against the Federal Register annex before any transaction. Everything in Part A marked ESTIMATE is an indicative market figure, **NOT A LIVE QUOTATION**, and no supplier is bound by it.

## Key Question 1 — What are the exact HTSUS codes and MFN duty rates for Turkish stone and tile?

### Takeaway
The widely repeated belief that "marble and travertine are duty-free at MFN" is **wrong for finished product**. Only raw/roughly-trimmed blocks and sawn marble blocks are free; worked marble, travertine and limestone under heading 6802 carry MFN rates of **1.9%–4.9%**, travertine specifically **4.2%**, and porcelain/ceramic tile **8.5%–10%**. All figures below were read directly from the USITC HTS database on 26 September 2026.

### Cited Findings — Chapter 25 (blocks and raw stone)
Source for all rows: [USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule, heading 2515, retrieved 26 Sep 2026](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=2515)

| HTSUS | Description | Unit | General (MFN) | Col. 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2515.11.00.00 | Marble/travertine, crude or roughly trimmed | m³, t | **Free** | $22.95/m³ |
| 2515.12.10.00 | Marble, merely cut into blocks/slabs of rectangular shape | t | **Free** | 13% |
| 2515.12.20.00 | Travertine, merely cut into blocks/slabs | t | **3%** | 50% |
| 2515.20.00.00 | Other calcareous monumental/building stone; alabaster (incl. limestone) | t | **3%** | 50% |

- Note the asymmetry: **marble blocks/slabs at 2515.12.10 are Free, but travertine at 2515.12.20 is 3%** — same heading, different statistical line. This is a real classification lever.

### Cited Findings — Chapter 68 (worked stone: slabs, tiles, finished product)
Source for all rows: [USITC HTS, subheading 6802.21, retrieved 26 Sep 2026](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6802.21) and [USITC HTS, subheading 6802.91, retrieved 26 Sep 2026](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6802.91)

| HTSUS | Description | Unit | General (MFN) | Col. 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6802.21.10.00 | Travertine, simply cut/sawn, flat or even surface | t | **4.2%** | 50% |
| 6802.21.50.00 | Other (marble, alabaster), simply cut/sawn | t | **1.9%** | 13% |
| 6802.91.05.00 | Marble/travertine/alabaster — **Slabs** | t | **2.5%** | 15% |
| 6802.91.15.00 | Marble/travertine/alabaster — Other | t | **4.9%** | 50% |
| 6802.91.20.00 | Articles of 6802.21.10 (travertine) dressed or polished, not further worked | t | **4.2%** | 50% |
| 6802.91.25.00 | Other (dressed/polished marble) | t | **3.7%** | 40% |
| 6802.91.30.00 | Alabaster | t | **4.7%** | 50% |

- Special-rate column for every one of the above lines reads `Free (A, AU, BH, CL, CO, D, E, IL, JO, KR, MA, OM, P, PA, PE, S, SG)` — **Türkiye is not in any of those programme lists**, so Turkish-origin stone pays the General/MFN rate. — [USITC HTS 6802.91](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6802.91)
- The HTS entry for 6802.21.50.00 carries a footnote cross-referencing **9903.90.08** in the Column-2 field. I could not determine what 9903.90.08 provides for in the time available. **FLAG: must be checked before entry.** — [USITC HTS 6802.21](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6802.21)
- Onyx is not a separate HTS line. Commercial "onyx" is calcareous (aragonite/banded calcite) and classifies with marble/alabaster in 2515/6802.21/6802.91, not with siliceous onyx-quartz. **Gap: no CROSS ruling retrieved to confirm; obtain a binding ruling before committing to a classification for onyx.**

### Cited Findings — Chapter 69 (ceramic and porcelain tile)
Source: [USITC HTS, subheading 6907.21, retrieved 26 Sep 2026](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6907.21) and [USITC HTS, subheading 6907.22, retrieved 26 Sep 2026](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6907.22)

**6907.21 — water absorption ≤ 0.5% (true porcelain):**

| HTSUS | Description | Unit | General (MFN) | Col. 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6907.21.10 | Unglazed | m² | **10%** | 55% |
| 6907.21.20.00 | Glazed; not over 3,229 tiles/m², faces bounded entirely by straight lines | m² | **10%** | 55% |
| 6907.21.30.00 | Glazed; largest surface area < 38.7 cm² (mosaic) | m² | **10%** | 55% |
| 6907.21.40.00 | Glazed; other | m² | **8.5%** | 55% |
| 6907.21.90 | Other | m² | **8.5%** | 55% |

**6907.22 — water absorption > 0.5% but ≤ 10% (standard ceramic floor tile):** identical structure and identical rates — 10% on .10/.20/.30, 8.5% on .40/.90.

- **Source conflict, resolved:** the aggregator [Dutiable.io](https://dutiable.io/hs-code/construction-materials/ceramic-floor-tiles) states "Ceramic Tile HS Code 6907.21 — 0% US Duty (2026)". This is **contradicted by the USITC HTS database itself**, which shows 10%. The USITC database is the primary source and is correct; the aggregator is wrong. Do not rely on tariff-aggregator sites for this trade.
- Türkiye again appears in none of the Special-rate programme lists for 6907. — [USITC HTS 6907.21](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=6907.21)

### Inferences
- A US importer's duty exposure is dominated by **product form, not material**. Shipping Turkish marble as blocks (2515.12.10) is MFN-free; shipping the same stone as finished polished slab (6802.91.05) costs 2.5%, and as a finished tile (6802.91.25) 3.7%. The tariff schedule systematically penalises Turkish value-added.
- Porcelain tile is the worst-treated category in this basket: 10% MFN before any additional tariff, versus 1.9–4.9% for stone.

### Gaps
- Heading 2516 (granite, sandstone, porphyry) was not retrieved — out of the four materials asked about, but relevant if the importer diversifies. Must be pulled separately.
- 6907.23 / 6907.30 / 6907.40 (water absorption > 10%; finishing ceramics) returned no rows in the keyword query and were not separately verified.
- No CBP CROSS classification rulings were retrieved. Classification of "filled and honed" travertine (6802.21.10 vs 6802.91.20 — both 4.2%, so duty-neutral) and of resin-backed / mesh-mounted stone tile (potentially 6802.10) remains unverified.

## Key Question 2 — What additional tariff currently applies to Turkish-origin goods?

### Takeaway
**CONFIRMED: an additional 12.5% ad valorem Section 301 duty applies to most Türkiye-origin goods, effective 12:01 a.m. ET, 24 July 2026, implemented by HTSUS 9903.05.79.** It stacks on top of the MFN rate. The Section 122 global tariff that followed the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling was terminated on the same date. Nothing in the retrieved exclusion descriptions covers stone or ceramics, so **stone and tile are subject to the full +12.5%**.

### Cited Findings
- **The operative HTSUS provision, read directly from the tariff schedule:** `9903.05.79 — "Except for products described in headings 9903.05.85–9903.05.92, articles the product of Türkiye, as provided for in U.S. note 52 to this subchapter"`, with the rate stated as **"the applicable duty rate plus 12.5%"** in both the General and Special columns, and "the applicable duty rate" (no add-on) in the Other/Column-2 column. — [USITC HTS, keyword Türkiye, retrieved 26 Sep 2026](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=T%C3%BCrkiye)
- The legal action is USTR's determination in the **Section 301 investigations of 60 economies for failure to impose and enforce a prohibition on importation of goods produced with forced labor**, published in the Federal Register on 28 July 2026 (document 2026-15181). — [Federal Register 2026-15181](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies); [USTR press release, July 2026](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations)
- USTR's own tiering: **10%** for economies with a forced-labor import ban or a reciprocal trade agreement (Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, **India**, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, UK); **10% or 12.5% net of MFN** for the **EU**, Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Switzerland; **12.5%** for all other investigated economies. — [USTR press release](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations)
- Türkiye is not named in the USTR press release; the 12.5% falls out of the residual "all other investigated economies" tier and is **confirmed independently by the HTS text of 9903.05.79** above.
- **REPORTED (secondary):** Türkiye received 12.5% rather than 10% "because the USTR found it had not adopted a qualifying import ban or made a commitment to Washington." — [Turkish Minute, 24 July 2026](https://www.turkishminute.com/2026/07/24/us-imposes-12-5-percent-tariff-on-turkish-goods-under-forced-labor-trade-action/)
- **Section 122 termination CONFIRMED by secondary source:** "The Section 122 tariffs expired and were replaced on July 24, 2026. The new Section 301 Forced Labor tariffs took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on July 24, 2026." — [RVIA, "Section 122 Tariffs Replaced By New Section 301 Forced Labor Tariffs"](https://www.rvia.org/news-insights/section-122-tariffs-replaced-new-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs)
- **One exclusion verified directly:** `9903.05.85` is a **transit grandfather clause**, not a product carve-out — "articles that were loaded onto a vessel at the port of loading and in transit on the final mode of transit prior to entry into the United States before 12:01 a.m. eastern time on July 24, 2026," dutiable at "the duty provided in the applicable subheading" with no add-on. — [USITC HTS 9903.05.85](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.05.85)
- **REPORTED exclusion categories (9903.05.86–9903.05.92, NOT individually verified):** "oil and gas, fertilizer, aircraft and parts, critical minerals, some food and goods already subject to national security tariffs, including automobiles, steel, aluminum and copper." — [Turkish Minute, 24 July 2026](https://www.turkishminute.com/2026/07/24/us-imposes-12-5-percent-tariff-on-turkish-goods-under-forced-labor-trade-action/). A second aggregator confirms exemptions are "code-specific," covering "goods covered by Section 232 actions and those on the exemption lists in the notice." — [ustariffrates.com/tariff-rates/turkey, last reviewed 24 Jul 2026](https://ustariffrates.com/tariff-rates/turkey)
- The 12.5% is **additive to MFN**, not a ceiling: the HTS text is "the applicable duty rate plus 12.5%." — [USITC HTS 9903.05.79](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=T%C3%BCrkiye)

### Inferences
- **Total duty rates currently in force for Turkish-origin goods (MFN + 12.5% Section 301):**
  - Marble blocks 2515.12.10 — 0% + 12.5% = **12.5%**
  - Travertine blocks/slabs 2515.12.20 — 3% + 12.5% = **15.5%**
  - Limestone 2515.20.00 — 3% + 12.5% = **15.5%**
  - Marble slabs 6802.91.05 — 2.5% + 12.5% = **15.0%**
  - Travertine tile, sawn 6802.21.10 — 4.2% + 12.5% = **16.7%**
  - Travertine tile, honed/polished 6802.91.20 — 4.2% + 12.5% = **16.7%**
  - Marble tile, polished 6802.91.25 — 3.7% + 12.5% = **16.2%**
  - Porcelain tile, glazed, standard 6907.21.20 — 10% + 12.5% = **22.5%**
  - Porcelain tile, glazed, "other" 6907.21.40 — 8.5% + 12.5% = **21.0%**
- Because no retrieved exclusion mentions chapters 25, 68 or 69, and the exclusions that are described are energy/agriculture/aerospace/Section-232-overlap in character, **the working assumption must be that stone and tile are fully subject to the 12.5%**. That is the conservative and almost certainly correct reading.
- **The single most commercially important unresolved point:** if the EU really is capped at "10% all-in *including* MFN" (as the RVIA summary states), then Spanish and Italian porcelain would enter at a total 10% while Turkish porcelain pays 22.5%. That would be a 12.5-percentage-point structural disadvantage for Turkey in the tile category — enough to kill the trade. **This must be verified against the Federal Register annex before any tile programme is committed to.**

### Gaps
- **federalregister.gov blocked the automated fetch** (302 redirect to unblock.federalregister.gov) on 26 Sep 2026, so the annex to FR 2026-15181 — the authoritative product exclusion list and U.S. note 52 — could not be read. **The importer must read U.S. note 52 to subchapter III of chapter 99 and the FR annex directly.** Try govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-28 as an alternative route.
- HTS subheadings 9903.05.86 through 9903.05.92 were not retrievable individually; their product scope is unknown.
- No CBP CSMS message on implementation of 9903.05.79 (reporting requirements, stacking order with other 9903 provisions) was located.
- Whether the Section 301 duty is assessed on the same customs value base as MFN (transaction value, FOB) was not separately confirmed, though there is no indication otherwise.
- **Re-verify on the date of transaction.** The IEEPA→122→301 sequence in 2026 shows this regime is unstable. A Section 301 action is also subject to periodic USTR review and to litigation.

## Key Question 3 — Does Section 232 apply, and are there AD/CVD orders on Turkish stone or tile?

### Takeaway
Section 232 metals tariffs do **not** reach natural stone or ceramic tile: the relevant provision is the 15%-by-weight de minimis under HTSUS 9903.82.03, and stone/tile contain no applicable metal. On AD/CVD, **natural stone and ceramic tile from Türkiye appear clean, but engineered quartz surface products from Türkiye are under live AD and CVD orders continued on 15 January 2026** and must be rigorously excluded from any product range.

### Cited Findings
- **Section 232 de minimis:** "HTS provision 9903.82.03 created a de minimis threshold: if steel or aluminum content falls below 15% by weight, the good may qualify for 0% Section 232 duty. For entries on or after April 6, 2026, certain derivative goods outside Chapters 72, 73, 74, and 76 may avoid Section 232 duties if the applicable steel, aluminum, or copper content is less than 15% of total product weight." — [Mallory Group, Section 232 15% de minimis explainer](https://www.mallorygroup.com/blog-posts/section-232-tariff-update-new-15-de-minimis-threshold-details-for-u-s-importers)
- Section 232 metals tariffs derive from Proclamations 10895 and 10896 of 10 February 2025 (25% ad valorem on steel/aluminium articles and certain derivatives), later expanded. — [Mallory Group](https://www.mallorygroup.com/blog-posts/section-232-tariff-update-new-15-de-minimis-threshold-details-for-u-s-importers); [CBP Section 232 FAQs](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/232-tariffs-aluminum-and-steel-faqs)
- **No search result connected Section 232 to stone or tile.** Natural stone slabs, stone tile and porcelain tile contain 0% steel, aluminium or copper by weight — far below the 15% threshold — and are classified in chapters 25, 68 and 69, outside chapters 72/73/74/76.
- **AD/CVD — quartz, LIVE ORDERS:** "Certain Quartz Surface Products From India and the Republic of Türkiye: Continuation of Antidumping and Countervailing Duty Orders," published **15 January 2026** (91 FR 1751), following Commerce and ITC determinations that revocation would likely lead to continuation or recurrence of dumping, countervailable subsidies and material injury. Original orders published 22 June 2020; sunset reviews instituted 1 May 2025. — [Federal Register 2026-00739, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00739/certain-quartz-surface-products-from-india-and-the-republic-of-trkiye-continuation-of-antidumping); [govinfo 91 FR 1751](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/FR-2026-01-15/2026-00739); [USITC determinations, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.usitc.gov/press_room/news_release/2025/er1208_67787.htm)
- **AD/CVD — ceramic tile:** the existing US order on ceramic tile is on **China** (A-570-108), and there are USITC proceedings on **ceramic tile from India** (investigations continued, May 2024). No US AD/CVD order or investigation on ceramic tile from Türkiye was found. — [USITC, ceramic tile from India, 31 May 2024](https://www.usitc.gov/press_room/news_release/2024/er0531_65247.htm); [CBP AD/CVD message 3032403, ceramic tile from China](https://www.customsmobile.com/adcvd/3032403)
- **Not US, but relevant to competitive reasoning:** the EU imposed definitive anti-dumping duties of up to 20.9% on Turkish ceramic tile. This is an EU measure with no US effect, but it explains why Turkish tile producers are pushing volume toward the US. — [European Parliament, Case AD 684](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/P-9-2022-003453_EN.html); [Global Compliance News](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2023/06/03/https-www-internationaltradecomplianceupdate-com-2023-06-01-eu-anti-dumping-and-anti-subsidy-duty-enforcement-increased-actions-against-imports-from-turkiye-_06012023/)
- No AD/CVD order or investigation on natural stone (marble, travertine, limestone, onyx) from Türkiye was found in any search.

### Inferences
- The AD/CVD boundary is the **engineered vs. natural** line, not the country line. Any product containing a resin/polymer binder with crushed quartz aggregate risks falling into the scope of the quartz surface products order. Slabs marketed as "quartz," "engineered stone," "agglomerate" or "compact surface" are out of bounds from Türkiye. Sintered stone / porcelain slabs have been the subject of scope disputes in this order in other jurisdictions — **get a scope ruling before importing any large-format sintered or compacted slab from Türkiye.**
- Because the US has no tile AD/CVD order against Türkiye while it does against China (and possibly India), Turkish tile's relative position on AD/CVD is favourable — which partially offsets the 12.5% Section 301 penalty versus China's identical 12.5%.

### Gaps
- The Commerce/ITA AD/CVD order list itself (access.trade.gov) was not queried directly; findings on "no order for stone/tile from Türkiye" rest on the absence of search results rather than on a positive database check. **Confirm with a direct search of the ITA AD/CVD Orders list and CBP's ADD/CVD case list before first import.**
- The current status of the India ceramic tile case (whether orders were imposed after the May 2024 continued investigations, and at what rates) is unresolved. This matters for competitive analysis, not for Turkish compliance.
- 9903.82.03's exact text and certification/reporting requirements were not read directly from the HTS.

## Key Question 4 — What are FOB Turkey prices for stone and tile?

### Takeaway
Indicative EXW Türkiye marble runs roughly **$40–140/m²** for 2 cm slabs depending on colour and rarity, and FOB Turkish porcelain **$6–16/m²** with premium slabs to $35/m². Both sets are published market guides from trade intermediaries, not quotations — they are directionally useful but should be treated as the upper-middle of a negotiable range. The most defensible single hard number available is IMIB's **$343.70 per tonne average export value** for all Turkish natural stone.

| Material | EXW USD/m² |

### Cited Findings — Aggregate Turkish export unit values (most reliable price evidence found)
- Turkish natural stone exports, first seven months of 2025: **3.3 million tonnes worth $1,135 million**, average **$343.70 per tonne** — up from **$318.11/tonne** in full-year 2024. — [Marmo Macchine International, citing IMIB/TurkStat](https://www.marmomacchineinternational.com/en/turkey-natural-stone-exports-up-5-4-in-the-first-7-months-of-2025/)
- Breakdown within that: finished and semi-finished **marble $562.5 million (+11.6%)**, **travertine $193.1 million (+9.1%)**, raw materials **$314.4 million (−5.4%)**. The mix is shifting away from raw blocks toward processed product. — [Marmo Macchine International](https://www.marmomacchineinternational.com/en/turkey-natural-stone-exports-up-5-4-in-the-first-7-months-of-2025/)
- June 2025 → June 2026: volume 2.12 → 2.94 million tonnes; value $490.4m → $693.28m (**+41.37%**). — [Marmo Macchine International](https://www.marmomacchineinternational.com/en/turkey-natural-stone-exports-up-5-4-in-the-first-7-months-of-2025/)
- Comparator: first nine months of 2024, Turkish natural stone exports $1.43 billion. — [StoneNews.eu](https://stonenews.eu/turkish-natural-stone-exports-reach-1-43-billion-in-the-first-nine-months-of-2024/)

### Inferences
- **A useful cross-check derived from the IMIB tonne figure.** At $343.70/tonne blended average and a density of ~2,700 kg/m³:
  - 2 cm slab = 54 kg/m² → implied **$18.56/m²**
  - 3 cm slab = 81 kg/m² → implied **$27.84/m²**
  - 1 cm tile = 25 kg/m² → implied **$8.59/m²**
  These are **far below** the SlabSite $40–140/m² guide. The gap is real and informative: the IMIB average is dragged down by raw blocks and commodity travertine going to regional markets, while the SlabSite figures are premium-colour slabs quoted to Western retail buyers. **The truth for a US-bound container of mid-grade commercial material sits between them — probably $25–55/m² FOB for 2 cm slabs.** An importer receiving a quote above $70/m² FOB for anything other than a genuinely rare colour should push back hard.
- Finish premiums (polished / honed / tumbled / brushed) are **not separately priced in any source retrieved**. Trade practice is that honed and polished are near-parity, tumbled adds a modest processing cost, and brushed/leathered sits between — but this is unsourced. **Gap.**
- Turkey's export price is rising fast (+41% year-on-year in value on +39% volume, i.e. unit value roughly flat to slightly up) — so historical price points degrade quickly. Anything older than six months should be discounted.

### Gaps
- **No FOB price found for limestone or onyx specifically.** Onyx in particular is a small, highly variable market where price is set per block; no public benchmark exists.
- **No sourced price differentiation between 2 cm and 3 cm**, and none between polished/honed/tumbled/brushed. These are the two variables the assignment asked for most specifically and neither is publicly disclosed.
- **No sourced per-container FOB prices.** All published figures are per m².
- **No FOB travertine tile price found.** Repeated searching returned only US retail listings. The derived $8.59/m² from the IMIB tonne average is the only defensible figure and it is an inference, not a quotation.
- Grade differentiation (Commercial / Standard / Premium / First Choice) is not priced in any retrieved source.

## Key Question 5 — What do US distributors and slab yards sell at, and what do customs unit values show?

### Takeaway
US consumer-facing sources put marble slab material at **$10–100+/sq ft**, but these are retail/fabricator-inclusive numbers, not slab-yard wholesale. **I could not obtain US customs unit values by HS code for Turkish origin** — USITC DataWeb and USA Trade Online both require authenticated access, and this is the single largest evidentiary gap in the price section.

### Cited Findings
- US marble countertop market, 2026: fabricated slab **$30–130 per sq ft** depending on grade and type; **material alone $10–100 per sq ft**. — [HomeGuide, Marble Countertops Cost 2026](https://homeguide.com/costs/marble-countertops-cost); [Angi, 2026 data](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-do-marble-countertops-cost.htm)

### Inferences
- The consumer-facing "$40–70/sq ft material" for Carrara is a **fabricator's or retail slab yard's price to a homeowner**, not the price a distributor charges a fabricator. Trade practice is that the slab-yard wholesale price to a fabricator is roughly 40–60% of that number. For mid-grade Turkish marble 2 cm polished, a realistic **US wholesale (slab yard → fabricator) range is $15–25/sq ft = $160–270/m², ESTIMATE, weakly sourced.** This is the weakest number in the whole model and the margin conclusions in Part C are highly sensitive to it.
- Even at the pessimistic end of that range, the spread over the ~$75/m² landed cost computed in Part C is very wide. **The margin in Turkish marble slabs is in the US distribution layer, not in the import arbitrage.** An importer who cannot access that distribution layer — i.e. who sells container-out to other wholesalers — will capture a fraction of it.

## Key Question 6 — Landed cost to Miami / Port Everglades, and resulting margins

### Takeaway
Landed cost runs roughly **1.35× FOB for marble slabs, 1.70× FOB for travertine tile and 1.83× FOB for porcelain tile** — the multiplier rises as FOB value falls, because freight and fixed customs costs are near-constant per container. Duty and freight together add **$20/m² on marble slabs, $8/m² on travertine tile and $7/m² on porcelain tile**. The model below is **ESTIMATE** throughout: the freight, THC, drayage and warehouse inputs are indicative market rates, not quotations.

### Cited Findings — freight and fee inputs
- **Ocean freight, 40 ft, Ambarlı or Mersin → US East Coast: roughly $3,000–4,750**, depending on season, carrier and fuel; transit **16–26 days**. — [Cumbaco, "Shipping from Turkey to USA in 2026"](https://cumbaco.com/insights/shipping-turkey-to-usa-2026/)
- A second 2026 data point: **40 ft Istanbul → Newark $5,500**. — surfaced via [iContainers, Turkey→US trade lane, updated August 2026](https://www.icontainers.com/trade-lane/turkey-to-us/)
- 2026 rate environment: "rates are up 15% year-over-year from Red Sea rerouting" and "peak season (Q4) includes 20–30% surcharges." — [Cumbaco](https://cumbaco.com/insights/shipping-turkey-to-usa-2026/)
- **Source conflict:** $3,000–4,750 versus $5,500 for essentially the same lane. The lower range is Mersin/Ambarlı→USEC generic; the $5,500 is a specific Istanbul→Newark quote and may include more services or a later, higher-rate snapshot. **I use $4,000 for a 40 ft and $3,000 for a 20 ft as mid-case, and flag that Q4 2026 shipments should be budgeted 20–30% higher.**
- **MPF (FY2026, effective 1 October 2025): rate 0.3464% ad valorem, minimum $33.58, maximum $651.50.** — [Federal Register, Customs User Fees Adjusted for Inflation in FY 2026, CBP Dec. 25-10](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/23/2025-13869/customs-user-fees-to-be-adjusted-for-inflation-in-fiscal-year-2026-cbp-dec-25-10); [Expeditors, CBP FY2026 fee changes](https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/cbp-announces-fee-changes-for-fiscal-year-2026); [Livingston International](https://www.livingstonintl.com/cbp-to-increase-user-fees-for-fiscal-year-2026-effective-october-1-2025/)
- **HMF: 0.125% ad valorem**, no minimum or maximum, applies to ocean imports at US ports. — [FreightFigures, US customs user fees 2026](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/us-customs-user-fees-mpf-hmf-2026). Statutory rate, unchanged.
- **UNSOURCED / ESTIMATE inputs used below** (indicative South Florida market rates, not quoted): destination THC Port Everglades $1,050 per 20 ft / $1,350 per 40 ft; marine cargo insurance 0.35% of CIF; customs broker entry fee $150; ISF filing $50; continuous customs bond amortised $50/entry (a $50,000 continuous bond typically costs $500–600/year and covers unlimited entries; a single-transaction bond would instead cost roughly 0.5% of value+duty and is materially worse for a repeat importer); drayage Port Everglades → local warehouse $550 per 20 ft / $650 per 40 ft including chassis and fuel; warehouse receiving/unload $300 per 20 ft / $400 per 40 ft.

### Model 1 — Marble slabs, 2 cm, polished, 20 ft container

### Inferences
- **Sensitivity.** The three variables that move the answer most, in order: (1) the actual US wholesale selling price, which is the least-sourced input; (2) the Section 301 12.5%, worth $6.88/m² on marble slabs and $1.13/m² on porcelain; (3) ocean freight, where a Q4 peak-season 30% surcharge adds $2.05/m² on a marble 20 ft and $0.96/m² on a porcelain 40 ft. FOB price negotiation moves marble a lot and tile very little.
- **Marble slabs are a value-dense trade, tile is a volume trade.** On marble, FOB is 73% of landed cost, so supplier negotiation is the lever. On porcelain, FOB is only 55% of landed cost, so freight contracts and container utilisation are the levers.
- **Weight, not volume, is the binding constraint on stone containers.** Every model above hits the payload ceiling, not the cubic ceiling. A 3 cm slab programme carries 1.5× the weight per m², so a 20 ft holds only ~290 m² — which raises the per-m² freight and fixed-cost allocation by about 50% ($10.46/m² of freight+fixed versus $6.82). 3 cm therefore needs a per-m² price premium of roughly $3.50 over 2 cm just to break even on logistics, before any material-cost premium.
- **Where Turkey wins:** travertine (Türkiye is the dominant global source and the US channel already exists); mid-grade beige/cream marble against Italian material at a fraction of the Italian price; limestone. **Where Turkey loses:** porcelain tile, where a 22.5% total duty against a possible 10% all-in for Spain and Italy and 10%-additional for India would be decisive; and premium white marble, where the Italian-name premium is the product and Turkish Muğla White does not command it.

### Gaps
- Every freight, THC, drayage and warehouse figure is an indicative market estimate. **Get three real quotes (a freight forwarder, a customs broker and a Port Everglades drayage company) before treating the model as decision-grade.**
- No Freightos or Drewry index reading for the Türkiye→USEC lane was obtained; the freight figures come from forwarder marketing pages, which tend to quote attractively.
- Container loading quantities (m² per 20 ft / 40 ft) are calculated from density and payload, not sourced. Actual loadings vary with crating and A-frame configuration.
- Demurrage, detention, per-diem and exam costs (a CBP or VACIS exam typically costs $300–1,200 and is a real risk on first-time importers) are not modelled.
- No Port Everglades–specific tariff schedule was consulted; Miami and Port Everglades THC and drayage differ.
