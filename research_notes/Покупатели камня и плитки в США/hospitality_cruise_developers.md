### Inferences
- The addressable volume is concentrated. A handful of towers (Edge House 608 units, Ombelle 754 units, Palm Tree 483 units, Icon Beach 350+6, Viceroy Brickell 476, Nexo 254, TwentySixth&2nd 233, NoBe PARC 232) represent thousands of bathrooms and kitchens each specifying stone or porcelain. - Buying windows are datable from the pipeline. Interior finish packages are normally bought out well after groundbreaking and before topping out, so the live 2026-2027 buyout candidates are the projects that broke ground in 2025-mid-2026 (Grand Hyatt, Villa Miami, Edge House, TwentySixth&2nd, Seventeen Gables, Pagani, Shorecrest, Icon Beach), while the 2027-groundbreaking projects (LILLI, Palm Tree, Mandarin Oriental North Tower) are 2028-2029 opportunities. This is an inference from construction sequencing, not a sourced schedule — do not present it as fact.
- Arquitectonica, Kobi Karp, ODP, CFE, Revuelta and Arquitectonica-affiliated interior houses (Meyer Davis, Adriana Hoyos, V Starr, Urban Robot, Charles & Co., Laura Gonzalez, ODA) recur across dozens of these projects. A small number of design firms therefore control specification across a very large share of the pipeline.

## Part A (continued) — Which general contractors build these projects, and who actually specifies vs. pays for the stone?

### Takeaway
The GC set is small and identifiable (Coastal, Moss, Suffolk, Balfour Beatty, Kaufman Lynn, MCM, John Moriarty), but in the standard South Florida high-rise model the GC is not usually the party that writes the cheque for the stone itself: the GC buys a tile/stone **subcontract** as a single line item, and the subcontractor buys the material. The architect/interior designer **specifies** the material; the developer **funds** it through an allowance or a GMP line; the subcontractor **purchases** it. Selling to the GC's procurement department alone will not produce an order.

### Inferences
- **Four distinct decisions, four different people.** (1) The architect/interior designer writes the specification (Arquitectonica, Kobi Karp, ODP, Meyer Davis, Adriana Hoyos, V Starr, Laura Gonzalez, Urban Robot appear repeatedly above). (2) The developer approves the finish budget/allowance and, on branded projects, must satisfy the hotel brand's design standards. (3) The GC's preconstruction and procurement staff (e.g. Coastal's Les O'Bryan / George Morales at budget and GMP stage, Elaine Fajardo at buyout) set the trade subcontract value and decide which subs are invited. (4) The **tile/stone subcontractor or fabricator issues the actual material purchase order and takes the import risk.** For a Turkish exporter, (4) is the customer and (1) is the gatekeeper; (2) and (3) are influencers.
- The highest-leverage play is a two-sided one: get the material **specified by name** with the designer (or accepted as an "or equal" to a specified Italian/Greek marble), and simultaneously be the quoted source for the two or three stone subcontractors who will bid that package. A spec without a sub who can price it, or a sub relationship without a spec, both fail.
- The allowance and GMP structure explains why price-per-square-foot landed and delivered matters more than FOB quarry price. The party choosing between suppliers is under a fixed contract value and will convert everything to installed cost, so quotes must be DDP-equivalent, with lead time and replacement-lot guarantees, to be usable.

## Part C — Cruise lines and ship interior contractors: is this channel realistically accessible?

### Takeaway
Honest answer: **largely closed for newbuilds, narrowly open for refurbishment, and open at all only through the outfitting contractor rather than the cruise line.** Newbuild interiors are delivered turnkey by European outfitters tied to European shipyards — Marine Interiors is a Fincantieri Group company and is described as the global leader in turnkey cruise interiors. The cruise lines do run supplier portals and Miami-based procurement organisations, including an "Outfitting Procurement" function at Royal Caribbean, but registering on a portal is not the same as being bought from. The realistic route is to become an approved material supplier to a drydock refit outfitter or to a South Florida marine tile installer.

### Inferences
- **Newbuild: effectively closed to a new Turkish stone supplier selling direct.** Interiors are contracted turnkey to European outfitters (Marine Interiors/Fincantieri, Bolici, De Wave) who are already integrated with the yards. The cruise line's own newbuild procurement buys the turnkey package, not the marble. Selling into a newbuild would mean becoming a tier-2/3 material supplier to an Italian or Finnish outfitter — possible, but it is a European industrial sales campaign, not a Miami one.
- **Drydock refurbishment is the only realistic opening, and even there the buyer is the outfitter or the tile installer, not the cruise line.** Atlantic Marine Interiors' P&O Ventura atrium ceramic tiling and Tuttle Tile's marine drydock tile work are the shape of the actual demand. Drydocks are scheduled years ahead and published, so approach can be timed.
- **Technical qualification is a hard gate.** Marine interior materials face IMO/SOLAS fire certification, weight limits and vibration/adhesion requirements that ordinary building-grade stone documentation does not satisfy. The prevalence of stone-*look* vinyl in cruise flooring is direct evidence that real stone is deliberately limited. Any pitch that does not lead with marine certification and weight-per-square-metre will not be read. (Inference from the certification and material evidence above; the specific certification requirements were not retrieved and should be verified.)
- **Portal registration is necessary but not sufficient.** Both Carnival and RCG operate registration mechanisms, but the sourced description of RCG's process — self-nomination plus commodity managers plus Seatrade/Marine Hotel Association introductions — indicates that the portal is a compliance filter and that real entry is through the category manager. For stone, the relevant RCG function is **Outfitting Procurement** (Miami), which is the correct title to target.
- Ranking of the three channels for a Turkish marble and travertine exporter, on the evidence gathered: Part A (South Florida developers/GCs/stone subs) is by far the strongest and has the clearest, datable demand; Part B (hotel FF&E purchasing agents) is probably a scope mismatch and should be de-prioritised until the hard-finishes question is answered directly; Part C (cruise) is a low-probability, long-cycle channel worth at most a limited refurbishment-focused effort.
