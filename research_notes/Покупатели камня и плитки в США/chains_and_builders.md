# US Retail Tile/Stone Chains and National Homebuilders as Buyers of Turkish Stone and Tile (September 2026)

Research date: 26 September 2026. All facts below carry an inline source. Where a fact could not be sourced it is listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

**Reliability warning used throughout:** import-data aggregators (ImportYeti, ImportGenius, Volza, ImportKey, Panjiva, Seair) resell US Customs bill-of-lading data. They are directionally useful for "does buyer X import from country Y", but their shipment counts are frequently inflated by double-counting and their company matching is loose. They are flagged as MEDIUM-LOW confidence wherever used. Privately held distributors (MSI, Emser, Bedrosians, Arizona Tile, Louisville Tile, Tile Outlets of America) publish no audited financials, so all scale figures for them are self-reported company marketing or third-party estimates.

## Q1. How do the largest US retail/wholesale tile and stone chains actually source, and is there a published vendor programme?

### Takeaway
The two biggest prizes — Floor & Decor and MSI — both run *direct-from-mill/quarry* import models with their own overseas sourcing relationships, which is exactly the channel a Turkish producer would want; but both buy in national-programme quantities from a deliberately small, long-standing vendor base (F&D: ~240 vendors covering 26 countries, largest single supplier = 11% of net sales), and neither publishes an open "apply to be a supplier" route. Turkish material is already deeply embedded across the sector — Emser's single largest supplier is a Turkish tile maker, and Tile Shop groups Turkey inside a "Europe" bucket that is roughly a third of its purchases — which means a new Turkish entrant is not opening a category, it is displacing an incumbent.

### Cited Findings

**Floor & Decor (NYSE: FND) — the largest specialty buyer**
- Operated **251 warehouse-format stores** (avg. 77,000 sq ft) plus 5 small-format design studios across **38 states** as of 26 Dec 2024; **4 distribution centres** (near Savannah, Houston, Los Angeles, Baltimore) plus an LA transload facility, with further DCs planned near Seattle and Baltimore in 2025–2026 — [FND FY2024 Form 10-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507079/000150707925000013/fnd-20241226.htm)
- Sources from **more than 240 vendors across 26 countries**; the merchandising team has **direct sourcing relationships with manufacturers and quarries**; the company explicitly states it buys the majority of product directly from manufacturers, "eliminating additional costs from exporters, importers, wholesalers and distributors" — [FND FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507079/000150707925000013/fnd-20241226.htm)
- **Largest single supplier = 11% of fiscal 2024 net sales**; no other supplier ≥10%. Approximately **18% of products sold in fiscal 2024 came from China**. Product range explicitly includes natural stone — [FND FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507079/000150707925000013/fnd-20241226.htm)
- F&D states many specialty flooring competitors "are too small to have the scale or the resources to work directly with suppliers" — i.e. direct import scale is framed as its moat — [FND FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507079/000150707925000013/fnd-20241226.htm)
- Published vendor requirements exist but are **compliance documents, not an application route**: a **Vendor Code of Conduct** and a **Supplier Social Accountability Compliance Program** set "the basic requirements that all vendors must comply with"; zero-tolerance violations stop POs and shipments, and resuming business requires "an audited, validated, corrective action plan as well as executive review and approval" — [F&D Vendor Code of Conduct (PDF)](https://flooranddecor.a.bigcontent.io/v1/static/fd-vendor-code-of-conduct); [FND IR — Sustainability: Processes](https://ir.flooranddecor.com/sustainability/processes)
- The Vendor Buying Agreement bars child/slave/prison/forced labour and human-trafficking-linked factories and requires ILO-standard conformance — [F&D Vendor Code of Conduct](https://flooranddecor.a.bigcontent.io/v1/static/fd-vendor-code-of-conduct)
- Sourcing is decided **centrally in merchandising at HQ (Atlanta)**. **Ersan Sayman** was promoted to **Executive Vice President of Merchandising** in Feb 2022 (19-year company veteran) — [Businesswire, 23 Feb 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220223006215/en/Floor-Decor-Holdings-Inc.-Announces-the-Promotion-of-Ersan-Sayman-to-Executive-Vice-President-of-Merchandising). **Lisa Laube**, previously EVP & Chief Merchandising Officer from 2012, was promoted to President in Feb 2020 — [Businesswire, 4 Feb 2020](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200204005065/en/Floor-Decor-Announces-Multi-Year-Employment-Agreements-for-Senior-Leadership-Team-and-Promotion-of-Lisa-Laube-to-President); [FND IR — Senior Leadership](https://ir.flooranddecor.com/about/senior-leadership)
- Below EVP level, category/VP-level merchandising roles exist — **Stacey Spilman, Merchandising Vice President** ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stacey-spilman-86866410/)); **Nick Taylor, VP of Merchandising** and **Tara Eagle, VP Online Merchandising** are listed on The Org ([theorg.com/org/floor-decor](https://theorg.com/org/floor-decor/org-chart/nick-taylor)) — MEDIUM confidence; The Org is a crowd-sourced org-chart aggregator, titles may be stale.
- F&D staffs a dedicated **"Merchandising Supplier Onboarding Specialist"** role, evidencing a formal internal onboarding pipeline for new vendors — [job posting aggregator, Workopia](https://workopia.io/jobs/eccc95e0ebaa74690e3a87edcdff62b5) — MEDIUM confidence (aggregated repost of an F&D listing).
- F&D also runs supplier-diversity support ("from getting registered and submitting certifications, to responding to RFPs and RFIs") via a Hire Ground portal — [flooranddecor.onhireground.com](https://flooranddecor.onhireground.com/) — note this is a *diversity/small-business* channel, largely US-domestic in orientation.

**The Tile Shop (NASDAQ→delisted: TTSH)**
- **140 stores in 31 states and DC** as of 31 Dec 2025; net sales **$336.8M in 2025** (down from $347.1M in 2024), **loss from operations of $5.8M**, and the company **exited Nasdaq** — [TTSH FY2025 Form 10-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552800/000155280026000006/c800-20251231x10k.htm); [StockTitan summary of the FY2025 10-K](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/TTSH/10-k-tile-shop-holdings-inc-files-annual-report-a189dbe7673e.html)

Turkey) = **36% of total purchases in 2019** vs 31% in 2018 — [TTSH FY2019 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552800/000155280020000005/ttsh-20191231x10k.htm); Europe = **32% of purchases in 2024** vs 29% in 2023 — [TTSH FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552800/000155280025000003/ttsh-20241231x10k.htm)

- The Tile Shop merchandises a **"Turkish Tiles" shop category** on its consumer site — [tileshop.com/shop/turkish-tiles](https://www.tileshop.com/shop/turkish-tiles)

- **Turkey is a named source country.** By 2008 MSI was "among the top importers of natural stone in the world, calling upon virtually every major source country... including India, Brazil, China, Turkey, Italy, Spain, and Mexico" — [MSI Company History](https://www.msisurfaces.com/corporate/company-history.aspx)
- Services **2,000+ fabricators** nationally and sells to tile retailers as a wholesale channel — [MSI blog, Dec 2025](https://www.msisurfaces.com/blogs/post/2025/12/24/how-msi-services-over-2000-fabricators.aspx); [MSI Tile Retailers segment](https://www.msisurfaces.com/business-segments/tile-retailers.aspx)

**Daltile / Mohawk Industries (NYSE: MHK)**
- Daltile operates **11 manufacturing facilities in North America, 5 Regional Distribution Centres, and over 250 Sales Service Centres** in the US, Canada and Puerto Rico — [Daltile Corporate & Investor Relations Profile](https://www.daltile.com/about-us/company-information/corporate-investor-relations-profile)

- **It does publish a supplier portal**: the **Mohawk Supplier Portal** at daltileb2bsuppliers.com, with a registration page, and user-add requests routed by email to separate contacts for **ceramic and glass** products and for **natural stone** products — [Mohawk Supplier Portal registration](https://www.daltileb2bsuppliers.com/Account/Register); [portal login](https://www.daltileb2bsuppliers.com/)

This is the clearest example in the sector of the "has a vendor portal ≠ onboards new overseas suppliers" distinction.

**Market context for Turkish material**
- Turkey's natural stone sector exported **$1.93 billion to 172 countries in 2024**, holding a **42% global share in processed marble and travertine**; the **United States was the top market in Jan–Jul 2025 at $273 million, +12.6% year on year** — [Marmo Macchine International, 2025](https://www.marmomacchineinternational.com/en/turkey-natural-stone-exports-up-5-4-in-the-first-7-months-of-2025/)
- CONFLICT TO FLAG: the same search surfaced a claim that "the United States alone imported $638 million in 2024 (+22%)" of Turkish stone — [Marmo Macchine International](https://www.marmomacchineinternational.com/en/turkey-natural-stone-exports-up-5-4-in-the-first-7-months-of-2025/). A $638M full-year 2024 figure is not consistent with $273M for the first seven months of 2025 at +12.6%; the two numbers likely cover different HS-code scopes (raw blocks vs processed). Do not present both as comparable.

### Inferences
- The realistic entry point for a new Turkish supplier is **not** Floor & Decor or MSI as a first customer. Both are already direct importers with mature Turkish relationships and buy at a scale where a new vendor must clear national-programme volume, EDI/compliance and audited-factory thresholds before a first PO. F&D's own filings frame direct sourcing as a scale moat, and its vendor documents are compliance gates rather than doors.
- Daltile/Mohawk is the least approachable of the group on tile, because it is primarily a **manufacturer** displacing imports with its own 11 North American plants; its import purchasing is a supplement, and its "supplier portal" is a transactional system for existing suppliers.
- The channel with genuine incremental Turkish demand is **natural stone specifically** (marble, travertine, limestone) rather than porcelain. Porcelain is where Turkey is already entrenched (Graniser, Yurtbay, Akgün, Seragres at Emser) and where Daltile/Mohawk's domestic plants and Spanish/Italian incumbents compete hardest.
- Mid-tier distributors (Louisville Tile, Bedrosians, Arizona Tile, Tile Outlets of America) are structurally more approachable than the top two, because they buy narrower programmes, publish the manufacturer lines they carry, and have less entrenched direct-sourcing infrastructure. Trade-show contact (Coverings, TISE/Surfaces) is the conventional route — but this is an inference from industry structure, not a sourced claim about any one of these companies' policies.

- **Emser and Bedrosians**: no published vendor/supplier programme pages found; no named category or sourcing managers found. NOT PUBLICLY DISCLOSED.

## Q2. Can a new, small overseas supplier realistically get onto these chains' vendor lists — approachability verdicts?

### Takeaway
Only the mid-tier distributors are realistically approachable at container volume by a new supplier; the two largest buyers (Floor & Decor, MSI) are effectively closed to a small first-time vendor, and Daltile/Mohawk is closed for a different reason (it manufactures rather than imports the bulk of what it sells).

### Cited Findings (verdicts and the evidence behind each)
- **Floor & Decor — HARD / effectively closed to a small new supplier.** Reasoning: ~240 vendors covering 26 countries is a deliberately consolidated base; one supplier already carries 11% of net sales; product must fill 251 stores through 4 (soon 6) DCs — [FND FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507079/000150707925000013/fnd-20241226.htm). There is a Vendor Code of Conduct and a social-accountability audit regime to clear — [F&D Vendor Code of Conduct](https://flooranddecor.a.bigcontent.io/v1/static/fd-vendor-code-of-conduct) — but **no public application route**. What it would take: national-programme fill rates across 38 states, audited factory, and sponsorship by a merchandising VP/category owner. Realistic only as a second-source/backfill for an existing SKU.
- **MSI — HARD / closed, and structurally a competitor as much as a customer.** MSI is itself the importer and distributor at 75,000 containers/yr across 50+ DCs and already sources Turkey directly — [MSI Company Profile](https://www.msisurfaces.com/corporate/company-profile.aspx); [MSI Company History](https://www.msisurfaces.com/corporate/company-history.aspx). No published supplier programme.
- **Daltile / Mohawk — HARD.** Vertically integrated: 11 North American plants — [Daltile IR profile](https://www.daltile.com/about-us/company-information/corporate-investor-relations-profile). Its portal is for suppliers already onboarded by a Daltile contact — [Mohawk Supplier Portal](https://www.daltileb2bsuppliers.com/Account/Register). Natural stone is the only plausible opening, since the domestic plants make ceramic/porcelain, and the portal does route natural-stone supplier administration separately.
- **The Tile Shop — MEDIUM, and the most transparent of the large chains.** ~200 suppliers (a wider base than F&D's 240 across a far smaller store count), top ten only 44% of purchases, an explicit stated preference for exclusive co-designed product, and Turkey already named in its sourcing disclosure — [TTSH FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552800/000155280025000003/ttsh-20241231x10k.htm). Counterweight: the company posted a **$5.8M operating loss on $336.8M of 2025 sales and exited Nasdaq** — [TTSH FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552800/000155280026000006/c800-20251231x10k.htm) — a shrinking, cost-cutting buyer is unlikely to expand its vendor base.
- **Emser Tile — MEDIUM-LOW.** Already one of the largest Turkish buyers in the US via Graniser and others — [ImportYeti](https://www.importyeti.com/company/emser-tile) (MEDIUM-LOW confidence). Being an established Turkey buyer cuts both ways: the country risk objection is already solved, but the incumbent slots are filled.
- **Bedrosians / Arizona Tile / Louisville Tile / Tile Outlets of America — MEDIUM to MEDIUM-HIGH, and the realistic first targets.** Evidence is thin and mostly structural: Louisville Tile publicly lists the manufacturer lines it carries, implying line-by-line vendor decisions rather than a closed global sourcing office — [Louisville Tile Featured Vendors](https://www.louisville-tile.com/featured-vendors/); Tile Outlets of America runs only three Florida supercenters on an outlet model — [tileoutlets.com](https://tileoutlets.com/).

### Inferences
- "Has a vendor portal" vs "onboards new overseas suppliers" splits cleanly here: **Daltile/Mohawk and D.R. Horton have portals that do not function as front doors**; **Floor & Decor has compliance documents but no portal**; **MSI, Emser, Bedrosians, Arizona Tile have neither**. Nobody in this set publishes an open overseas-supplier application.
- Because none of the top chains publishes an application route, the actual entry mechanism in this industry is relationship-led: trade shows (Coverings, TISE), agents already selling into those merchandising teams, or being brought in as a qualified second source when an incumbent fails on price or lead time. This is an inference from the absence of published routes, not a sourced statement of any company's policy.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence was found on how any of these chains actually adds a new overseas vendor in practice (minimum volumes, sample/testing protocol, audit provider, payment terms, first-order size). NOT PUBLICLY DISCLOSED.
- Arizona Tile: no verdict evidence at all beyond its inclusion as a peer. Treat the MEDIUM verdict above as an inference by analogy, not a finding.

## Q3. How do national homebuilders with large Florida operations actually buy finish materials, and are they a sellable target?

### Takeaway
Large builders genuinely do run national purchasing organisations and do sign direct national supply agreements with manufacturers — Lennar's LP Building Solutions agreement is a documented example — but the published evidence shows those national agreements concentrated on structural and branded commodity products, while purchasing authority for trade-executed scopes sits at division level with procurement agents, and buyer-facing finish selection runs through design studios stocked from pre-approved supplier agreements. For tile and stone specifically, no builder in this set was found to publish a direct materials-supply route for a foreign stone producer. The practical target is the installing flooring subcontractor and the builder's regional/division procurement agent, not corporate HQ.

### Cited Findings

**Purchasing structure — evidence that it is split national/division**
- **Lennar** states in its 10-K that it "realizes cost efficiencies through its national purchasing programs and production of value-engineered homes" — [Lennar FY2024 Form 10-K (PDF)](https://investors.lennar.com/~/media/Files/L/Lennar-IR-V3/documents/earnings-releases/len-4q24-10-k.pdf)
- Lennar signs **direct nationwide supply agreements with manufacturers**: a nationwide partnership and supply agreement with **LP Building Solutions** covering LP SmartSide Trim & Siding and structural solutions, announced April 2024 — [Businesswire, 10 April 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240410890486/en)
- Lennar staffs a **"Director of National Purchasing Operations"** role — [job posting aggregator, Workopia](https://workopia.io/jobs/ae064ad3b27aa644d528c6e50ab6c05a) — MEDIUM confidence (aggregated repost).
- **D.R. Horton** built its national purchasing function around **regional purchasing managers whose role is to get divisions onto corporate vendor deals** — established mid-2002 with four regional purchasing managers covering 47 divisions in 20 states — [Builder Magazine, "Supply Chain Initiatives: Power Purchasing"](https://www.builderonline.com/building/operations/supply-chain-initiatives-power-purchasing_o/). The division count is long out of date; the *structure* (corporate deals, divisional adoption) is the durable point.
- D.R. Horton operates a **Vendor Extranet** at ve.drhorton.com — [ve.drhorton.com](https://ve.drhorton.com/) — a login-gated system for vendors it has already contracted, not an open application. D.R. Horton also appears in supplier-diversity portal directories — [Diversity411 listing](http://diversity411.com/diversity/supplier-diversity-portals/d-r-horton-inc-supplier-diversity-program/) — LOW-MEDIUM confidence (third-party directory).

### Inferences
- **The premise in the brief is broadly correct, with one nuance.** For tile and stone specifically, no builder in this set publishes a direct materials-supply route, and the only formal trade-onboarding systems found (HB NEXT Sequence at D.R. Horton and Dream Finders; KB Trade Partners Online) are subcontractor prequalification systems. That is consistent with finishes being bought as installed labour-and-material packages from flooring subs. **The nuance:** these builders demonstrably *do* sign direct national manufacturer agreements (Lennar–LP; KB Home's named brand partners), so "builders never buy direct" is too strong. The pattern is that direct national agreements are struck with large branded manufacturers who can supply every division, and everything else flows through installing trades.
- **Therefore the real target for a Turkish producer is two-tier**: (1) the Florida flooring/tile installation subcontractors who hold the labour-and-material contracts with Lennar, D.R. Horton, Pulte, Taylor Morrison, GL Homes and Dream Finders; and (2) the distributors those subs buy from (MSI, Emser, Bedrosians, Louisville Tile, Florida Tile, Tile Outlets of America). A builder's national purchasing team is realistically reachable only *after* a supplier can already demonstrate installed performance through those channels.
- **The division-level Florida procurement agent is a real, addressable person** — Pulte's West Palm Beach Senior Procurement Agent posting proves the function is staffed in-state — but that role's leverage is over trade contracts and division adoption of corporate deals, not over originating a foreign materials vendor.
- Lennar's FY2026 guidance cuts, falling ASP and ~12% incentive load mean builders are in cost-compression mode. That is genuinely double-edged: it raises interest in a lower-cost source, and it simultaneously raises the bar on reliability, since a builder cutting incentives cannot absorb a late container.
