# Kraft Paper Manufacturers in India — Current Producers (Sept 2026) and Export Capability to Africa

**Research date: 25 September 2026.** All statements below carry the publication/verification date of the source. Where a source is undated or older than ~18 months, it is flagged. Nothing here is invented; unverified items are explicitly marked.

**Critical methodological warning for the report writer:** The Indian kraft paper sector is the single worst category in Indian B2B listings for trader/manufacturer conflation. IndiaMART, TradeIndia, ExportersIndia and Alibaba listings for "kraft paper manufacturer India" are dominated by traders and converters. Several companies in this dossier appear simultaneously as "Manufacturer", "Trading Company" and "Exporter" on PaperIndex profiles — that flag itself is evidence of mixed manufacturer+trading activity, not proof of milling. I have classified conservatively.

---

## Key Question 1: Who genuinely manufactures kraft paper in India at export scale?

### Takeaway
The large BSE/NSE-listed integrated mills the brief names (JK Paper, West Coast, ITC PSPD, TNPL, Andhra, Seshasayee, Century, Emami, Khanna, Satia, Kuantum, Naini) are overwhelmingly **writing & printing paper, coated packaging board, newsprint and tissue** producers — NOT brown kraft/testliner mills. The genuine Indian kraft/testliner export capability sits in a second tier of specialist recycled-kraft mills, chiefly in **Meerut/Muzaffarnagar/Saharanpur (UP)** and **Vapi (Gujarat)** clusters: Paswara Papers, Shree Ajit Pulp & Paper, Ashoka Pulp & Paper, Genus Paper & Boards, plus Ruchira Papers (largest listed dedicated kraft capacity) and Star Paper Mills (virgin specialty kraft only). Confusing these two tiers is the main risk in this sourcing exercise.

## Key Question 3: Kraft product taxonomy — do NOT conflate. The Indian BF convention.

### Takeaway
"Kraft paper" in India almost always means **recycled testliner/fluting graded by Burst Factor**, sold in a 14–40 BF band — a fundamentally different product and a different grading language from European/Scandinavian **virgin kraftliner**. A buyer quoting "kraftliner" and an Indian mill quoting "22 BF kraft" are not discussing the same paper.

### Cited Findings — the BF convention
- **GSM** = weight per square metre; higher GSM = heavier/thicker sheet — [U-PACK](https://www.upack.in/blog/post/GSM-and-BF-of-Kraft-Paper)
- **Bursting Factor (BF) is a DERIVED INDEX expressing bursting strength relative to grammage**, i.e. BF ≈ bursting strength (in appropriate units) ÷ GSM. It therefore **normalises strength for weight**, which is why Indian mills quote the pair "GSM + BF" and why BF alone tells you nothing about sheet weight. **"Burst Factor predicts bag rupture resistance better than GSM"** — [PaperIndex Academy, Understanding GSM and Burst Factor](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/understanding-gsm-and-burst-factor-a-specifiers-guide-to-paper-bag-strength/)
- Underlying test methods: **ISO 2758 (international) / TAPPI T 403** for bursting strength; ISO 536 (grammage); ISO 287 / TAPPI T 412 (moisture) — [PaperIndex capability matrix, 6 October 2025](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/kraft-paper-manufacturers-capability-matrix-compare-gsm-bf-bst-moisture-control-and-certifications-without-guesswork/); [PaperIndex sourcing guide, 22 January 2026](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/the-ultimate-mill-direct-kraft-paper-sourcing-guide/)
- **Typical BF bands by application: retail grades BF 16–20; cement grades BF 30–38+** — [PaperIndex Academy](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/paper-bag-raw-material-grades-gsm-and-burst-factor-explained/)
- **Absolute burst-strength equivalents (kPa):** typical kraft sack performance **200–300 kPa minimum burst**; lighter packaging grades **100–150 kPa**; reference conversion **35 PSI ≈ 241 kPa** — [PaperIndex capability matrix, 6 Oct 2025](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/kraft-paper-manufacturers-capability-matrix-compare-gsm-bf-bst-moisture-control-and-certifications-without-guesswork/). **These kPa figures are the bridge between Indian BF quotes and European/ISO specifications and should be used to translate.**
- **Moisture: target 6–8%, TAPPI T 412. A mill quoting 7% ±0.5% demonstrates superior process control; ±2% or wider signals inconsistency.** GSM tolerance typically **±2–5%** — [PaperIndex capability matrix, 6 Oct 2025](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/kraft-paper-manufacturers-capability-matrix-compare-gsm-bf-bst-moisture-control-and-certifications-without-guesswork/). One Indian mill listing specifies **"maximum 8% moisture content"** — [Kovai Maruthi, TradeIndia](https://www.tradeindia.com/products/plain-kraft-paper-4275113.html)
- **Named GSM/BF grade ladder actually published by an Indian mill** — [Gayatri Paper Mill kraft specifications](https://gayatripapermill.com/index.php/kraft-paper-specifications/):
  - **Fluting Paper: 140–250 GSM @ 18 & 20 BF**
  - **Test Liner: 140–250 GSM @ 22 & 24 BF**
  - **Kraft Liner: 140–250 GSM @ 28 & 35 BF**
  This is the cleanest published illustration of how the three grades separate by BF at identical GSM in the Indian convention.
- Another Indian mill publishes the full BF ladder as discrete SKUs: **BF 14 / 16 / 18 / 20 / 22 / 25 at 120–200 GSM**, alongside separate **Sack Kraft Paper** and **Wrapping Paper** lines — [Pinax Paper Mills, PaperIndex](https://www.paperindex.com/profile/pinax-paper-mills-pvt-ltd/22061817/25610)
- **Recycled kraft dominance:** "In India, recycled Kraft paper is among the widely utilized types, and plays a crucial role in manufacturing multi-ply corrugated boxes… 3-ply, 4-ply (litho laminated), 5-ply, and 7-ply boxes" — [search synthesis of Indian kraft grade sources](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/paper-bag-raw-material-grades-gsm-and-burst-factor-explained/)
- Confirmation from producers themselves: Shree Ajit is **"100% waste paper as raw material"** — [src](https://www.shreeajit.com/products); Ashoka is **"100% Recycled waste paper based Kraft paper"** — [src](https://www.ashokapapermills.com/); Paswara is **"100% recycled"** — [src](https://www.paswara.com/company-profile/). By contrast Star Paper is **"a completely virgin pulp-based mill"** — [src](https://papermart.in/premiumisation-over-volume-star-papers/) and JK Paper's packaging board is **"100 percent pure virgin grade, with no use of recycled board"** — [src](https://papermart.in/jk-paper-boosts-packaging-portfolio/).
- Grade families referenced in the sourcing literature: **Kraft Linerboard vs Testliner vs Fluting** are treated as three distinct comparison categories — [PaperIndex sourcing guide, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/the-ultimate-mill-direct-kraft-paper-sourcing-guide/). PaperIndex maintains structurally separate mill directories for **"Linerboard (Unbleached Kraft) / Kraftliner"** and **"Linerboard (Recycled) / Testliner"** — [kraftliner mills](https://www.paperindex.com/companies/paper-manufacturers/linerboard-unbleached-kraft-kraftliner/4966/6); [testliner mills](https://www.paperindex.com/companies/paper-manufacturers/linerboard-recycled-testliner/4963/6)

### Taxonomy for the report (definitions are industry-standard; the Indian-practice annotations are sourced above)
- **Virgin (unbleached) Kraftliner** — made from fresh softwood sulphate pulp. Highest strength per gram, best moisture resistance and printability, most expensive. In India this is a **rarity**: Star Paper is virgin but deliberately avoids kraftliner; JK Paper's board is virgin. Most "kraft liner" offered by Indian traders at BF 28–40 is either semi-virgin or imported.
- **Recycled Kraft / Testliner** — made from recovered paper (OCC/waste). This is **the Indian default product** and what Paswara, Shree Ajit, Ashoka and most of the cluster actually make. Lower and more variable strength; BF is the control variable. Suits general corrugated cases, secondary packaging, non-critical stacking.
- **Semi-virgin** — recycled furnish reinforced with a proportion of virgin pulp, often only in the top ply of a multi-layer sheet. Genus states it uses "recovered as well as virgin fibre". Paswara/Shree Ajit's **multi-layer/triple-wire** construction is exactly the mechanism: a better top ply over cheaper recycled back plies. Suits buyers needing a printable or higher-strength face at recycled cost.
- **Kraft Paper (generic wrapping/bag)** vs **Kraftliner/Testliner (corrugated facing)** vs **Fluting / corrugating medium (the wavy middle ply)** — different jobs: liner takes burst, tensile and print; fluting takes **compression (CCT/ring crush)** and is graded lower on BF (18–20 BF in the Gayatri ladder) precisely because burst is not its function. **Do not accept a fluting BF quote as a liner quote.**
- **Sack Kraft** — high-stretch, high-TEA, high-porosity virgin kraft for valve/cement/flour sacks, typically 70–100 GSM, requiring BF 30–38+ in the Indian convention. Only **Pinax Paper Mills** was found naming a sack kraft line. **Africa context: sack kraft is a large, separate African market — Africa imported 283K tons of sack kraft in 2024, up 11% YoY; Tanzania alone consumed 124K tons and produced 125K tons (28% of African output, twice Kenya's)** — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/sack-kraft-paper-africa-market-overview-2024/). If the Botswana requirement is sacks rather than corrugated, the Indian supplier set changes almost completely.
- **White-top Kraftliner / White Kraft Liner** vs **Brown Kraftliner** — white-top carries a bleached top ply for high-quality print (retail-ready packaging); brown is the commodity. Shree Krishna **trades** "White Kraft Liner (BF 28)" and "Virgin Top Liner (BF 30 to 40)" — [src](https://www.indiamart.com/shree-krishna-papermills-industries/profile.html); Paswara and Shree Ajit offer **coloured/golden shades** ("Ajit Gold Shade") rather than true white-top. **No Indian mill in this research was confirmed producing genuine white-top kraftliner.**
- **Specialty kraft** — absorbent kraft (laminate/decor substrate: Star Paper, Naini, JK), MG kraft/poster (Pakka), electrical insulation kraft (Shree Krishna), masking, ribbed kraft, barrier/poly-coated. **These are NOT interchangeable with packaging kraft and their presence in a mill's catalogue is not evidence of liner capability.**

### Inferences
- **A European-style RFQ will fail with Indian mills.** The workable RFQ format is: GSM + **BF (with the ISO 2758 kPa equivalent stated as a cross-check)** + ring crush/CCT for fluting + Cobb(60) + moisture % with tolerance + ply construction (single/multi-layer) + furnish (recycled/semi-virgin/virgin) + shade. Ask for a mill test certificate per reel batch.
- **BF is scale-free, so "22 BF" at 100 GSM and at 300 GSM are very different absolute strengths.** Any comparison table in the final report must pair the two numbers and never rank mills on BF alone.
- **Cobb (water absorptiveness) is the most commercially important spec no Indian mill publishes.** For Botswana — a long humid sea leg via Durban/Walvis Bay plus inland transit — Cobb and moisture control matter more than headline BF. Neither Paswara, Shree Ajit, Ashoka nor Genus publishes a Cobb figure. This must be extracted in the RFQ.

### Gaps
- **No Indian mill reviewed publishes ring crush (RCT), CCT or Cobb values.** Sources reviewed carry BF and moisture only.
- The 6–8% moisture and kPa-equivalent norms come from PaperIndex Academy (a marketplace's own content), not from a standards body or a mill datasheet. Directionally credible, but not authoritative.
- I could not obtain a single genuine mill datasheet PDF for any Indian kraft producer.

## Key Question 5: Reel/roll specs, packaging, MOQ

### Takeaway
This is the weakest-evidenced area of the entire research. Essentially no Indian kraft mill publishes reel width, reel diameter, core diameter, reel weight, packaging spec or MOQ. Only machine deckle widths were obtainable.

### Cited Findings
- **Machine deckles (the upper bound on reel width):** Ashoka PM1 **2700 mm**, PM2 **3600 mm**, PM3 **3000 mm**, PM4 **3900 mm** — [src](https://www.ashokapapermills.com/). Shree Ajit PM-1 finished deckle **2740 mm** (triple wire), PM-2 **2700 mm** (single wire) — [src](https://www.shreeajit.com/about)
- **Only reel dimension found anywhere:** an IndiaMART product line "Finished Paper Reel – 117 cms Diameter" from Indo Afrique Paper Mills — [src](https://www.indiamart.com/indo-afrique-papermills/) — i.e. **~1170 mm reel diameter**. Low confidence (B2B listing, single data point).
- **Container/logistics:** the sourcing guide recommends **6–10 kg of desiccant per 40-ft container** for paper shipments to prevent container rain — [PaperIndex sourcing guide, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/the-ultimate-mill-direct-kraft-paper-sourcing-guide/). The guide includes a "logistics protection" section but **no tonnage-per-container figures.**
- **MOQ: NOT PUBLICLY DISCLOSED — ask manufacturer.** Confirmed absent from: Paswara ([src](https://www.paswara.com/company-profile/)), Shree Ajit ([src](https://www.shreeajit.com/products)), Ashoka ([src](https://www.ashokapapermills.com/)), the PaperIndex sourcing guide and the capability matrix ([src](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/the-ultimate-mill-direct-kraft-paper-sourcing-guide/), [src](https://www.paperindex.com/academy/kraft-paper-manufacturers-capability-matrix-compare-gsm-bf-bst-moisture-control-and-certifications-without-guesswork/)).
- **Packaging spec (wrapping, edge protection, headers, palletisation/eye-to-sky vs eye-to-side): NOT PUBLICLY DISCLOSED by any producer reviewed.**

### Inferences
- With deckles of 2700–3900 mm, mills will slit to customer widths; standard Indian corrugated widths of ~1000–1600 mm will be trim-optimised against the deckle. **A single-container buyer ordering an odd width will either pay a trim penalty or be fitted into another customer's trim plan — this is the main hidden cost at 1-container volumes.** Not sourced; flag as a negotiation point rather than a finding.
- **The 1-container/month requirement is likely BELOW the comfortable MOQ of Paswara (450 TPD) and Ashoka (900 TPD).** A 900 TPD mill produces a container's worth of paper in under an hour; a monthly container is a rounding error on its order book. Genus's kraft line at 300 TPD, Shree Ajit at ~300 TPD equivalent, or Orient Board (125 TPD) and Indo Afrique (45 kt) are proportionally more likely to engage seriously with the volume. **This is an inference from capacity figures, not a sourced statement about anyone's MOQ.**
- Practical consequence: for ~1 container/month, either (a) accept a trader/consolidator as intermediary — which is what the Vitara Papers→Jewel Paper Products Botswana link appears to be, or (b) negotiate a 12-month frame contract with monthly call-offs so the mill books an annual tonnage rather than a monthly container.

### Gaps
- **No reel width, reel diameter, core diameter (76 mm / 152 mm etc.), reel weight, or packaging specification was obtained for ANY Indian kraft mill.** All of it must come from direct RFQ.
- **No tonnage-per-container figure was obtained from any source.** I deliberately did not estimate one.
- Volza's kraft-paper export pages, which would carry per-shipment weights and so reveal real order sizes, returned **HTTP 403** on direct fetch (both the India-export and Botswana-import pages) — [blocked](https://www.volza.com/p/kraft-paper/export/export-from-india/). Paid access needed.

### Inferences
- **India's African kraft channel is East African (Tanzania/Kenya/Uganda/Nigeria/Ethiopia), not Southern African.** That is a direct consequence of geography and shipping: the India→East Africa leg is short and direct, whereas Southern Africa requires longer routings, typically transhipped via Durban. A Gaborone buyer is therefore asking Indian mills to serve a lane they do not currently run at volume — expect higher freight, fewer sailings and less mill familiarity with the documentation.
- **For Gaborone (landlocked), the real constraint is the last leg, not the mill.** The route is Mundra or Nhava Sheva → Durban (or Walvis Bay) → road/rail ~1,000+ km to Gaborone. The Botswana–South Africa paper flows evidenced above confirm this corridor is in routine use. Durban transhipment plus SACU inland haulage will likely exceed the FOB paper cost differential between candidate mills — **so mill selection should weight reliability, moisture/Cobb control and documentation quality over FOB price.**
- **Gujarat mills (Shree Ajit, Best Paper Mills, Shah, Vidhisha) have a structural edge for this lane** — Mundra and Nhava Sheva are close, avoiding 1,300+ km of Indian inland haulage that UP mills (Paswara, Ashoka, Genus, Star, Orient Board) must absorb.
- **Paswara is the only candidate with any documented African commercial activity** (Ethiopia named, South Africa referenced twice albeit weakly, Mundra port named, FSC certified, 150 kt capacity). On the evidence available it is the strongest starting point despite the UP location. Shree Ajit is the strongest on verifiable technical documentation and port position but has zero African evidence.
- The presence of three established kraft/corrugated players in Gaborone (General Packaging, K-Square, Rona Acreage) means a new importer is entering a served market. This is commercially relevant: it implies existing supply lines the buyer will be priced against, and it identifies General Packaging Industries as both the likeliest customer and the likeliest competitor.

### Gaps
- **No documented Indian-mill→Botswana kraft paper shipment was found.** The only India→Botswana link is via a trading LLP.
- **Volza and ImportGenius shipment-level detail is paywalled; direct fetches returned HTTP 403.** Per-shipment weights, consignees, HS codes and ports for India→Southern Africa kraft could not be obtained. **A paid Volza/Panjiva query on HS 4804 / 4805 India→Botswana, South Africa, Namibia, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique is the single highest-value next step in this research.**
- **No named export manager, Africa/overseas sales manager, or LinkedIn profile was found for any Indian kraft mill.** The only named individuals obtained are directors (Shree Ajit board; Star Paper MD Madhukar Mishra) and one generic-inbox owner (shilpi@ashokapapermills.com). Annual reports, IPMA member directory pages and trade-fair exhibitor lists were not successfully mined for export-sales personnel.
- **No DGFT, Ministry of Commerce or Indian customs primary export statistics were accessed.** All trade figures here are from Volza/IndexBox/IPMA secondary reporting.
- No SADC/Botswana import tariff, SACU duty, or BOBS/standards requirement for kraft paper was researched — potentially material to landed cost.
- CIN numbers: none found for any company.
- Bindals Papers and Agarwal Duplex: no data at all.
