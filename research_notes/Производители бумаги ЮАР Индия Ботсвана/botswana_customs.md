# Botswana Import Customs Regime for Paper Products (Newsprint, Kraft Paper, Kraftliner, Sack Kraft) — as of September 2026

> **Headline for the report writer:** the single most important finding is that **every tariff line in headings 48.01, 48.04 and 48.05 carries a "free" General (MFN) rate of duty in the SACU Common External Tariff**, as published by SARS on **2026-08-28**. This means the duty differential between South African origin and Indian origin for these specific products is **zero percentage points** — the difference between the two origins is entirely in *formalities, permits, VAT base and logistics cost*, not in duty. There is no SACU–India preference in force (only Terms of Reference signed 12 August 2026), but for these HS codes no preference is needed. Verified Botswana VAT = 14% (BURS's own customs web page still says 12% — it is stale; see conflict section).

---

## Q1. Correct 8-digit HS codes for newsprint, kraft paper/kraftliner, sack kraft, testliner (Botswana/SACU)

### Takeaway
Botswana applies the SACU Common External Tariff, which is identical in structure and numbering to the South African Schedule No. 1 Part 1. At the level published in the tariff book, headings 48.01, 48.04 and 48.05 are **not broken down into national 8-digit lines** — SACU stops at the 6-digit international subheading (e.g. 4804.11) plus a single check digit (CD), so the operative code entered on a declaration is written as `4804.11` with check digit `3`. Only where SACU has created national splits (as it has in 48.02 and 48.10) do genuine 8-digit codes such as `4802.57.30` appear. For all of 48.01, 48.04 and 48.05 there are **no 8-digit splits** — the 6-digit subheading is the full statutory line.

### Classification notes that decide which line applies (verbatim from the legal notes in the same tariff, page dated 2026-08-28)
- **"Newsprint"** (Chapter Note 4): uncoated paper for printing newspapers, ≥50% by mass of total fibre content of mechanical/chemi-mechanical wood fibres, unsized or very lightly sized, Parker Print Surf (1 MPa) on each side exceeding 2.5 µm, **40–65 g/m²**, and only in strips/rolls of width **>28 cm** or sheets with one side >28 cm and the other >15 cm.
- **"Kraft paper and paperboard"** (Chapter Note 6): ≥80% by mass of total fibre content of fibres obtained by the **chemical sulphate or soda** processes.
- **"Kraftliner"** (Subheading Note 1, for 4804.11 and 4804.19): machine-finished or machine-glazed, ≥80% chemical sulphate/soda wood fibre, **in rolls**, mass **exceeding 115 g/m²**, and meeting minimum Mullen bursting strength: 115 g/m² → 393 kPa; 125 → 417; 200 → 637; 300 → 824; 400 → 961 kPa (interpolated/extrapolated for other masses).
- **"Sack kraft paper"** (Subheading Note 2, for 4804.21 and 4804.29): machine-finished, ≥80% sulphate/soda fibre, **in rolls**, mass **60–115 g/m²**, and meeting either (a) Mullen burst index ≥3.7 kPa·m²/g with stretch >4.5% cross direction and >2% machine direction, or (b) minimum tear/tensile per the table (e.g. 70 g/m²: tear MD 830 mN, MD+CD 1 790 mN; tensile CD 2.3 kN/m, MD+CD 7.2 kN/m).
- **"Testliner"** (Subheading Note 5, for 4805.24 and 4805.25): made wholly or mainly of pulp of recovered paper/paperboard; may have a surface layer of dyed paper or of bleached/unbleached non-recovered pulp; Mullen burst index ≥2 kPa·m²/g.
- **Heading scope limit** (Chapter Note 8): headings 48.03–48.09 apply **only** to paper in strips/rolls of width **>36 cm**, or sheets with one side >36 cm and the other >15 cm. Narrower reels/sheets fall out of 48.04/48.05 and must be classified elsewhere (often into dutiable lines) — a real commercial trap for slit reels.
- **Tie-break** (Chapter Note 7): where goods answer to two or more of headings 48.01–48.11, classify in the heading occurring **last** in numerical order. So a recycled liner that could read as kraft would go to 48.05 over 48.04.

### Inferences
- A buyer specifying "kraftliner" must hold mill test data (Mullen burst, grammage) to defend 4804.11/4804.19; a "kraft liner" made mainly from recycled fibre is legally **testliner** and belongs in 4805.24/4805.25. Since both are free, the revenue consequence is nil, but the declaration must still be correct because Botswana/SACU penalties attach to incorrect classification irrespective of duty loss.
- The practical classification risk is *width*, not fibre: reels slit to ≤36 cm drop out of 48.04/48.05 entirely (Note 8) and can land in dutiable 48.02 or 48.11 lines.

### Gaps
- Botswana does not publish its own tariff book online. One BURS-adjacent source states the tariff book "is available for sale at all regional Customs and Excise offices, but it is not currently available via the internet" — [Botswana Import Tariffs (Privacy Shield / former export.gov Country Commercial Guide)](https://www.privacyshield.gov/ps/article?id=Botswana-Import-Tariffs). Therefore the codes and rates above are taken from the SARS publication of the **same** SACU Common External Tariff. If absolute certainty on a Botswana-side declaration is required, the codes should be confirmed against Botswana's ASYCUDA/e-Customs tariff file via a licensed Botswana clearing agent or the BURS Customs & Excise regional office in Gaborone.
- Botswana Trade Portal hosts the SACU Schedules 3, 4, 5 and 6 and Schedule 1 Part 2A/2B as PDFs (e.g. [Schedule No 3](https://www.botswanatradeportal.org.bw/kcfinder/upload/files/Schedule%20No%203.pdf), [Schedule No 1 Part 2A](https://botswanatradeportal.org.bw/kcfinder/upload/files/Schedule%20No%201%20Part%202A.pdf)) but I could not locate a Schedule 1 Part 1 file on that portal (direct URL returned 404), and the Part 2A copy there is dated **2016-03-31**, i.e. significantly out of date versus the SARS file.

## Q2. For each HS code: Botswana import duty rate, VAT rate, other levies or surcharges

### Takeaway
**Every line in 48.01, 48.04 and 48.05 is duty-free at the General (MFN) rate**, so an Indian-origin kraftliner or newsprint shipment pays **0% customs duty** in Botswana, the same as a South African one. VAT at **14%** is the only material tax. There are no anti-dumping, countervailing or safeguard duties in force on Chapter 48 goods in SACU.

### Cited Findings — no trade remedies on paper
- SACU Schedule No. 2 ("Anti-dumping, countervailing and safeguard duties on imported goods"), **dated 2026-09-18**, contains the chapter headers **item 210.00 "Pulp of wood or of other fibrous cellulosic material; waste and scrap of paper or paperboard; paper and paperboard and articles thereof"** and **item 210.02 "Paper and paperboard; articles of paper pulp, of paper or of paperboard"** with **no tariff lines listed beneath them** — i.e. the categories exist in the schedule's framework but carry zero operative anti-dumping entries. ### Inferences
- Because General = SADC = free for these codes, **origin certification is commercially irrelevant to the duty outcome** for 48.01/48.04/48.05. It remains procedurally relevant (see Q5/Q6) and would become relevant instantly if the product were reclassified into 48.02, 48.03, 48.10 or 48.11.
- Since Botswana duty is 0% for these codes, import VAT is levied on essentially the same base from either origin: VAT = (transaction value + insurance + freight) × 14%, with duty of zero added for the Indian consignment.

### Gaps

## Q3. CRITICAL — SACU: what it actually means for South African vs Indian origin, quantified

### Takeaway
SACU makes South African goods **duty-free and origin-document-free and permit-free** on entry to Botswana, and it means Indian goods pay the **Common External Tariff** — which for newsprint/kraftliner/sack kraft/testliner happens to be **zero**. So the quantified duty difference between the two origins for these specific products is **0 percentage points**. The real, quantified differences are: (i) an **import permit** is required for goods entering Botswana directly from outside SACU but **not** for goods entering from within SACU; (ii) the VAT base for a non-SACU consignment includes customs duty (here zero, so no effect) while the SACU consignment's base is value + insurance + freight; (iii) the non-SACU consignment additionally needs origin documentation and, if railed/trucked across South Africa, a **Removal-in-Bond / transit** regime with security.

### Cited Findings
- "No customs duties and excise duties are charged on trade between Botswana and South Africa, Lesotho, Namibia, and eSwatini, as these five countries constitute the Southern African Customs Union." — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Botswana – Other taxes (reviewed 09 Jan 2026)](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/botswana/corporate/other-taxes)
- "Importations between SACU countries are free of Customs and Excise duty." All external imports face a Common Customs external tariff, with revenue pooled and shared among member states — [Lloyds Bank Trade, Botswana customs (July 2026)](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/botswana/customs)
- Botswana "is a member of the Southern African Customs Union (SACU), applying the Common External Tariff (CET) shared with South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho, and Eswatini. While goods traded between SACU members are generally free of customs duties, imports from non-member countries are subject to the CET." — [Botswana Import Tariffs (former export.gov CCG mirror)](https://www.privacyshield.gov/ps/article?id=Botswana-Import-Tariffs)
- BURS's own assessment formulas make the SACU/non-SACU distinction explicit:
  - From SACU member states (Lesotho, Namibia, South Africa, Eswatini): **Customs duty 0%**; VAT = (transaction value + insurance + freight) × rate.
  - From outside SACU: **Customs duty = transaction value × applicable tariff rate**; VAT = (transaction value + customs duty + insurance + freight) × rate.
  — [BURS, Assessment and payment (Customs duty and import VAT)](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/payments-of-customs-duty-and-import-vat/assessment-and-payment)
- BURS VAT guidance on valuation: for SACU members "the value for VAT purposes will include insurance and freight"; for other imports the value must include "all taxes and duties payable, and the cost of insurance and freight." — [BURS, VAT – Imports](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/tax/value-added-tax/imports)
- Permit asymmetry: "With the exception of goods originating in Malawi, import permits are required for goods entering Botswana directly from outside the SACU and are obtainable from the Department of International Trade, in the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry." Import permits are **not** required for goods entering directly from within SACU — [US ITA Country Commercial Guide, Botswana – Import Requirements and Documentation (last updated 8 Dec 2025)](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation); same wording at [Privacy Shield / export.gov mirror](https://www.privacyshield.gov/ps/article?id=Botswana-import-requirements-and-documentation)
- SACU comprises "South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini" — [Business Day, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/2026-08-12-india-and-sacu-revive-talks-on-preferential-trade-agreement/)
- Cargo from South African ports to Botswana is "commonly forwarded under RIB (Removal in Bond) by road to Gaborone for further clearing and final delivery" — [DSV Botswana](https://www.dsv.com/en/countries/africa/botswana)

### Quantified bottom line — % added to CIF value (paper of 48.01/48.04/48.05)

| Origin | Customs duty | Import VAT | Total added to CIF | Net cost after VAT recovery (VAT-registered importer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **South Africa** (SACU) | 0% | 14% of (value + insurance + freight) = **14% of CIF** | **+14.0%** of CIF | **+0%** (VAT is recoverable input tax) |
| **India** (third country, MFN/General) | **0%** (all lines free) | 14% of (value + duty + insurance + freight) = 14% × (CIF + 0) = **14% of CIF** | **+14.0%** of CIF | **+0%** (VAT is recoverable input tax) |

### Inferences
- **The commonly assumed "SACU advantage" does not exist as a tariff advantage for these paper grades.** Anyone modelling an Indian kraftliner landed cost should not add duty. The Indian origin's disadvantage is administrative and logistical: an MTI import permit, origin documentation, ocean freight to Durban/Walvis Bay, a Removal-in-Bond transit through South Africa with security, and roughly 1 300 km of inland haul — not customs duty.
- Because VAT applies identically at 14% on both and is recoverable, on a pure tax basis the two origins are **tax-neutral**. The choice collapses to ex-mill price, freight, lead time and working-capital cost of the VAT float.
- The SACU revenue-sharing arrangement means duty collected on the Indian consignment (here, nil) would be pooled centrally rather than retained by Botswana — relevant to political economy, not to the buyer's cost.
- If the Indian consignment were reclassified into a dutiable line (e.g. narrow reels falling into 48.02 at 15%), the SACU/third-country gap would become material immediately: +15% duty plus 14% VAT on the duty-inclusive base = roughly +31% over CIF versus +14% for South African origin. This is the main quantified classification risk.

### Gaps
- Whether Botswana requires a **separate SACU origin declaration (DA 59-equivalent)** for South African goods, or accepts the supplier's invoice declaration alone, is **not confirmed from a primary BURS source**. Within a customs union goods move on the invoice plus a SAD 500, and no preferential certificate is needed, but the precise BURS practice at Tlokweng should be confirmed with the clearing agent.
- The SACU 2002 Agreement text and the current revenue-sharing formula were not fetched; SACU Secretariat documents would be the primary source if the report needs the institutional detail.

## Q4. Botswana VAT: current rate, payable at the border, recoverable as input tax

### Takeaway
The standard rate is **14%**, reached after a documented zig-zag (12% → 14% on 1 April 2021 → temporarily 12% from 3 August 2022 → back to 14% on 1 April 2023). Import VAT is payable **when the goods are cleared through customs**, is calculated on a CIF-plus-duty basis, can be **deferred to the 25th of the following month** against security, and **is recoverable as input tax by a VAT-registered importer — but only after it has actually been paid.**

### Cited Findings
- Standard rate **14%** on all supplies not exempt or zero-rated; VAT imposed on importation of goods; registration threshold BWP 1 million. Page reviewed **09 January 2026** — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Botswana](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/botswana/corporate/other-taxes)
- **Rate history:** Botswana raised VAT to 14% in **April 2021**; reduced it temporarily from 14% to 12% **from 3 August 2022**; extended the temporary 12% to **31 March 2023**; the Ministry of Finance confirmed in the **2023 Budget Speech on 6 February 2023** that the rate would return to 14% on **1 April 2023** — [vatcalc, Botswana temporary VAT cut ends 1 April 2023](https://www.vatcalc.com/botswana/botswana-temporary-vat-cut-to-12-till-31-march-2023/); [Sovos, Botswana VAT rate to increase from 12% to 14%](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/vat/botswana-vat-rate-to-increase-from-12-to-14/)
- **Timing:** "VAT liability on imports arises when the goods are cleared through Customs." — [BURS, VAT – Imports](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/tax/value-added-tax/imports)
- **Base:** taxable value on import includes "all taxes and duties payable, and the cost of insurance and freight"; for SACU-origin imports the value "will include insurance and freight" — [BURS, VAT – Imports](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/tax/value-added-tax/imports)
- **Valuation basis is CIF/transaction value:** "Transaction value is: the price actually paid or payable for the goods when sold for export to Botswana" — [BURS, Assessment and payment](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/payments-of-customs-duty-and-import-vat/assessment-and-payment)
- **Deferment:** the legislation "makes provision for deferment of VAT due on imports where the importer provides adequate security for the tax due", or where the Commissioner is satisfied as to the importer's payment record; monthly statements are generated with payment due by the **25th of the following month**. Payment is otherwise due "at time of importation or within a month of importation for traders who have registered to operate deferred account facility with BURS", in Pula, by "cash or bank guaranteed Cheque" — [BURS, VAT – Imports](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/tax/value-added-tax/imports); [BURS, Assessment and payment](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/payments-of-customs-duty-and-import-vat/assessment-and-payment)
- **Input tax recovery:** "Input tax for imported purchases should be claimed only after payment has been made." Claiming input tax on deferred VAT before payment attracts "interest and penalties", and if discovered after 4 months the claimant "risks losing input tax recovery rights" — [BURS, VAT – Imports](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/tax/value-added-tax/imports)
- Legislative churn to be aware of: a **Value Added Tax Act, 2026 commenced 1 July 2026**; the **VAT (Amendment) Act, 2025** extended VAT to non-resident remote (digital) services and introduced a mandatory Electronic Fiscal Device (EFD) regime; a **July 2026 BURS notice** brought private medical services into the VAT net from **1 August 2026** — [vatcalc, Botswana VAT on non-resident digital services](https://www.vatcalc.com/botswana/botswana-vat-on-non-resident-digital-services/)
- Zero-rated supplies include exports; exempt supplies include financial services, residential rentals, public transport, medical and dental services, education — [LookupTax, Botswana VAT guidelines](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/botswana-vat-guidelines-indirect-tax)

### CONFLICT: BURS web pages still state 12%
- **BURS's own customs pages give 12%**: the assessment formulas on [BURS, Assessment and payment](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/payments-of-customs-duty-and-import-vat/assessment-and-payment) are written as VAT = (value + insurance + freight) × **12%** for SACU imports and (value + duty + insurance + freight) × **12%** for non-SACU imports. Several third-party pages repeat this, e.g. "Goods imported into Botswana from outside the SACU market are liable to 12% VAT" and [Lloyds Bank Trade (July 2026)](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/botswana/customs) which states "All imported goods face a 12% Value Added Tax".
- **Contradicted by** [PwC (reviewed 09 Jan 2026)](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/botswana/corporate/other-taxes) and [US ITA CCG (updated 8 Dec 2025)](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation), both stating 14%, and by the documented statutory reversion to 14% on **1 April 2023** confirmed in the 2023 Budget Speech — [vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/botswana/botswana-temporary-vat-cut-to-12-till-31-march-2023/).
- **Which is current:** **14%.** The 12% figures on BURS's customs web pages and on aggregator sites are residue from the 3 Aug 2022 – 31 Mar 2023 temporary reduction (or from the pre-April-2021 rate) and have not been edited. The *formulas* on the BURS page remain correct; only the percentage substituted into them is stale. The authoritative confirmation for a specific shipment should be the BURS VAT public notice/rate schedule or the VAT Act 2026 as in force — worth requesting in writing from BURS given the 1 July 2026 commencement of the new Act.

### Inferences
- For a VAT-registered Botswana importer the 14% is a **cash-flow cost, not a cost of goods** — with the deferment account, VAT paid on a 10th-of-the-month clearance is due on the 25th of the following month, which can be close to or after the point of onward sale. Without deferment, cash goes out at the border and comes back only on the next VAT return.
- The "claim only after payment" rule interacts badly with deferment: an importer using a deferment account must not claim the input tax in the period of importation but in the period of actual payment. This is a common and expensive error worth flagging to the buyer.

### Gaps
- The exact **statutory instrument / section reference** fixing 14% under the Value Added Tax Act 2026 was not retrieved. **UNKNOWN** — to be confirmed from the VAT Act 2026 as published in the Botswana Government Gazette, or a BURS public notice.
- Whether the 2026 Act changed the import-VAT deferment rules or the 25th-of-following-month deadline is **NOT CONFIRMED**.
- The value and form of security required for a VAT deferment account (bank guarantee amount, typically a multiple of monthly VAT) is **NOT PUBLICLY DISCLOSED** in the sources found — to be established with BURS VAT Division.

## Q5. Preferential treatment for India → Botswana?

### Takeaway
**No preference is in force.** There is no SACU–India PTA: the two sides only signed **Terms of Reference** for negotiations on **12 August 2026**, with text-based talks to begin within a month and a one-year target. India's Duty-Free Tariff Preference scheme runs the other way (it gives LDCs access to *India's* market and Botswana is not an LDC anyway). For these particular HS codes the absence of preference costs nothing, because the General rate is already free.

### Cited Findings
- SACU and India **signed the Terms of Reference to guide negotiations towards a Preferential Trade Agreement on 12 August 2026 in New Delhi**; formal text-based negotiations were to commence within one month, with both teams targeting conclusion within one year — [IMPRI, India–SACU PTA Negotiations (2026)](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/india-south-africa-trade-agreement/); [Business Standard / Capital Market, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/india-and-the-southern-african-customs-union-sign-terms-of-reference-towards-a-preferential-trade-agreement-126081300712_1.html); [LogUpdate Africa](https://www.logupdateafrica.com/amp/trade/india-sacu-sign-tor-for-pta-negotiations-1360286)
- The move **revives talks after five rounds of negotiations between 2002 and 2010 ended without a deal** — [Business Day, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/2026-08-12-india-and-sacu-revive-talks-on-preferential-trade-agreement/); [allAfrica, Aug 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608170699.html)
- The envisaged PTA is to cover eight chapters: trade in goods and preferential market access, rules of origin and origin procedures, customs procedures and trade facilitation, trade remedies including a bilateral safeguard mechanism, SPS, TBT, dispute settlement, and legal/institutional/horizontal provisions — [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/india-south-africa-trade-agreement/)
- Scale of the relationship: India's exports to SACU totalled **USD 7.5bn in 2025/26** and imports **USD 9.2bn**; India–South Africa bilateral trade was **USD 15.56bn in 2025–26**, down 13.55% — [Business Day, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/2026-08-12-india-and-sacu-revive-talks-on-preferential-trade-agreement/); [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/india-south-africa-trade-agreement/)
- The SACU tariff's preference columns are **EU/UK, EFTA, SADC, MERCOSUR and AfCFTA** only — there is **no India column** — [SARS Schedule No. 1 Part 1, dated 2026-08-28](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/SCEA1964/Legal-LPrim-CE-Sch1P1Chpt1-to-99-Schedule-No-1-Part-1-Chapters-1-to-99.pdf)

### Inferences
- **State plainly in the report: as of September 2026 Indian-origin goods enter Botswana at the General (MFN) rate of the SACU CET. No preferential certificate of origin (Form A, EUR.1, SADC certificate) can reduce the rate, and none is available.** A SACU–India PTA is at most in early text-based negotiation, with no signed agreement, no ratification and no tariff schedule.
- India's Duty-Free Tariff Preference (DFTP) scheme is a *unilateral Indian* concession to Least Developed Countries for exports **into** India. Botswana is an upper-middle-income country, not an LDC, and in any case DFTP is irrelevant to duties charged **by** Botswana. Do not present DFTP as relevant to this transaction.
- AfCFTA has a rate column in the SACU tariff (already applied — the 2026-08-28 schedule shows AfCFTA rates such as 6%, 4%, 2% where General is 15%, 10%, 5%). India is not an African state and cannot use it. For these paper codes AfCFTA is moot since General is already free.

### Gaps
- I did not find the **India–Botswana bilateral** picture separately (there is no India–Botswana bilateral trade agreement to my knowledge, and none surfaced); a search of Botswana MTI/Ministry of Trade and Entrepreneurship trade-agreement lists would confirm. **Treat "no India–Botswana bilateral preference" as unverified-but-strongly-indicated** rather than sourced.

## Q6. Documentation required for import clearance

### Takeaway
The operative declaration is the **SAD 500** (bill of entry), lodged by a registered clearing agent, with commercial invoice, packing list, transport document and origin evidence. For an Indian consignment an **import permit from the Department of International Trade (Ministry of Trade / Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry) is required**; for a South African consignment it is not. **No phytosanitary certificate applies to paper** (paper is a manufactured cellulose product, not a plant product) — but wooden pallets and crating are a separate ISPM 15 question.

### Cited Findings
- "Customs duties are paid using the prescribed **SAD 500** form, a declaration document formally known as a **bill of entry**." — [US ITA Country Commercial Guide, Botswana – Import Tariffs (last updated 8 Dec 2025)](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-tarifs)
- "The **SAD 500 form is the only document required for external trade**." — [US ITA CCG, Botswana – Import Requirements and Documentation (8 Dec 2025)](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation)
- BURS: goods imported or exported "must be declared to BURS (Customs & Excise Division) against an appropriate **CUSTOMS FORM** with supporting documents (**import licence, invoice, certificates of origin** etc.)", stating the customs procedure code for the transaction type (importation for home use, temporary importation, warehousing, etc.) — [BURS, Customs Clearance](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/customs-clearance)
- BURS publishes a **SAD Guide** for completion of the declaration — [BURS SAD Guide (PDF)](https://www.burs.org.bw/phocadownload/customs_and_excise_downloads/SAD_Guide.pdf)
- Document set listed for Botswana imports: **inland bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, customs import declaration, VAT deferral form**; import permits from the relevant Ministry/Department mandatory for goods entering directly from outside SACU (except Malawi); importers must register with BURS for a TIN — [Lloyds Bank Trade, Botswana customs (July 2026)](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/botswana/customs)
- Travellers/personal baggage use **Form J** (baggage declaration) — not applicable to commercial paper cargo but useful context — [Botswana border posts, procedures and customs](http://selfdrive4x4.co.za/botswana_border_posts.html)
- BURS publishes a formal overview notice, "**Customs & Excise Procedures for Manufacturers and Traders**" — [BURS e-Customs document library](https://ecustoms.burs.org.bw/TFBSEW/docs/Customs%20&%20Excise%20Procedures%20for%20Manufaturers%20and%20Traders.pdf) — and "**Importation of Goods into Botswana: Overview**" — [BURS e-Customs](https://ecustoms.burs.org.bw/TFBSEW/docs/IMPORTERS%20%20EXPORTERS.pdf). (Both are image/compressed PDFs I could not reliably text-extract; the report should cite them as the primary BURS procedural references to be read by the buyer's agent.)
- Further practical document checklists: [FIDI Botswana Import Customs Guide (Feb 2025)](https://www.fidi.org/sites/default/files/public/2025-02/BOTSWANA%20Import%20%E2%80%93%20FIDI%20Customs%20Guide.pdf); [R&R, Documents required for customs clearance in Botswana](https://www.shiprrexp.com/global_shipping_documents/Botswana.html)

### Which origin form — direct answer
- **SAD 500 is the customs declaration, not an origin certificate.** It is required for every consignment from either origin.
- **South African origin:** no preferential certificate of origin is needed, because movement is *intra*-customs-union — there is no preferential rate to claim, duty is simply not levied between SACU members ("Importations between SACU countries are free of Customs and Excise duty" — [Lloyds Bank Trade](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/botswana/customs)). A supplier's invoice with an origin statement is the normal evidence. A **SADC Certificate of Origin** is the instrument for SADC (non-SACU) preferences, e.g. Zimbabwe or Zambia — not needed for South Africa.
- **Indian origin:** **no preferential origin document exists or helps.** Form A (GSP) is not applicable — GSP is a scheme of developed importing countries, and Botswana operates no GSP. EUR.1 applies only to EU/UK and EFTA agreements. A **non-preferential Certificate of Origin** (chamber-of-commerce issued in India) is nonetheless routinely required as a supporting document for the SAD 500 and for the MTI import permit — BURS expressly lists "certificates of origin" among supporting documents — [BURS, Customs Clearance](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/customs-clearance).

### Phytosanitary and other certificates
- **No phytosanitary certificate is required for paper or paperboard.** No source in this research imposes SPS control on Chapter 48 goods; Botswana's SPS controls attach to plants, plant products, animals and foodstuffs. Paper is a processed manufacture.
- **Wood packaging is the real SPS exposure:** solid-wood pallets, cases and dunnage from India are regulated wood packaging material internationally under ISPM 15 and must be heat-treated/fumigated and marked. **NOT CONFIRMED for Botswana specifically in this research** — flag as a check with the Botswana Ministry of Agriculture / Plant Protection Division, and specify ISPM-15-marked or plastic/pressed-wood pallets in the Indian purchase contract as a precaution.
- Controlled-substance permits (BoMRA) are irrelevant to paper — [US ITA CCG](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation)

### Gaps
- Whether BURS requires a **Certificate of Origin for paper specifically**, or only where a preference or a permit is claimed, is not stated in the sources. Practically, the MTI import permit application and the SAD 500 for a non-SACU consignment will both call for one.
- I could not text-extract the two authoritative BURS procedural PDFs (scanned/compressed). Their detailed document lists remain **unverified in my notes** even though the documents themselves are the right primary sources.

## Q7. Is an import permit or licence required for paper in Botswana? Which authority?

### Takeaway
**Yes for India, no for South Africa.** Botswana requires an import permit for goods entering directly from **outside SACU** (Malawi excepted), issued by the **Department of International Trade** in the trade ministry. Permits are described as non-transferable and "usually granted upon request", i.e. an administrative formality rather than a quantitative restriction — but it is a hard document requirement and a lead-time item.

### Cited Findings
- "With the exception of goods originating in Malawi, import permits are required for goods entering Botswana directly from outside the SACU and are obtainable from the **Department of International Trade, in the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry**." Permits "are not transferable" and "are usually granted upon request" — [Privacy Shield / export.gov mirror of the Botswana CCG](https://www.privacyshield.gov/ps/article?id=Botswana-import-requirements-and-documentation)
- The current US ITA CCG names the issuing body as the "**Department of International Trade in the Ministry of Trade and Entrepreneurship**", says permits are "**required for goods entering from outside SACU**", are "non-transferable" and "typically granted upon request", and that "import permits **not required** for goods entering directly from within SACU". Last updated **8 December 2025** — [trade.gov, Botswana – Import Requirements and Documentation](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation)
- "Import permits from the relevant Ministry or Department are mandatory for goods entering directly from outside SACU (except from Malawi)." — [Lloyds Bank Trade (July 2026)](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/botswana/customs)
- BURS lists "import licence" as a supporting document to the customs declaration, and instructs traders to verify before importing whether their goods face "prohibitions or restrictions" — [BURS, Customs Clearance](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/customs-clearance)
- The legal basis for import permits is Botswana's **Control of Goods** legislation (the same framework that generates instruments such as the [Control of Goods (Intoxicating Liquor (Levy)) Regulations](https://www.botswanatradeportal.org.bw/kcfinder/upload/files/Control%20Of%20Goods%20(Intoxicating%20Liquor%20(Levy))%20Regulations.pdf) hosted on the Botswana Trade Portal), alongside the **Customs and Excise Duty Act, Cap 50:01** — [Customs and Excise Duty Act, Chapter 50:01 (PDF)](https://assets.tobaccocontrollaws.org/uploads/legislation/Botswana/Botswana-Customs-Excise-Duty-Act-native.pdf)

### Inferences
- Note the ministry has been renamed across sources (Ministry of Trade and Industry → Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry → Ministry of Trade and Entrepreneurship). For a 2026 application the buyer should approach the **Department of International Trade** under its current ministry name; the department is the constant.
- Because the permit is granted on request rather than rationed, the practical impact on an Indian shipment is **lead time and a document to be in hand before the goods reach the border**, not market access. It should be applied for at order placement, not at arrival — a missing permit at Tlokweng is a storage/demurrage event.

### Gaps
- **Permit validity period, fee, processing time and whether one permit can cover multiple shipments: NOT PUBLICLY DISCLOSED in the sources found.** To be confirmed directly with the Department of International Trade, Gaborone.
- Whether paper appears on any specific restricted/controlled schedule under the Control of Goods regulations (as opposed to the general non-SACU permit requirement) was **not verified** — the Botswana Trade Portal's measures database would be the place to check.

## Q10. Requirements on the Botswana importer: registration, TIN, customs client code, BURS registration

### Takeaway
An importer needs to be a registered Botswana entity, hold a **BURS Trader Identification Number (TIN)**, be VAT-registered if turnover exceeds BWP 1 million (which it will for commercial paper volumes), and either appoint a **licensed clearing agent** or be separately registered with BURS to self-clear. Deferment of duty/VAT requires a separate BURS-approved deferment account backed by security.

### Cited Findings
- "Importers must register with BURS for a **TIN (Trader Information Number)**." — [Lloyds Bank Trade, Botswana customs (July 2026)](https://www.lloydsbanktrade.com/en/market-potential/botswana/customs)
- BURS operates a **Trader Identification Number (TIN)** system, and goods "should be cleared through registered Clearing Agents **or by companies registered to carry out their own Customs clearance**" — [BURS, Customs Clearance](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/customsexcisemain/customs-clearance)
- VAT registration threshold **BWP 1 million** — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Botswana (reviewed 09 Jan 2026)](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/botswana/corporate/other-taxes)
- Deferment requires that "the importer provides adequate **security** for the tax due", or that the Commissioner is satisfied as to the importer's payment history; statements are settled by the **25th of the following month** — [BURS, VAT – Imports](https://www.burs.org.bw/index.php/tax/value-added-tax/imports)
- Import permits for non-SACU goods are obtained by the importer from the Department of International Trade and are **non-transferable** (so they must be in the importer's own name) — [trade.gov, Botswana – Import Requirements and Documentation](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation)
- No exchange-control obstacle to paying an Indian supplier: "Botswana has no foreign exchange restrictions, making it relatively easy to access foreign currency for transactions" — [trade.gov, Botswana – Import Requirements and Documentation](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-import-requirements-documentation); foreign exchange controls were abolished in **February 1999** — [Privacy Shield / export.gov CCG mirror](https://www.privacyshield.gov/ps/article?id=Botswana-import-requirements-and-documentation)

### Inferences
- Botswana's TIN is the functional equivalent of South Africa's customs client code: it is the identifier quoted on the SAD 500. I found **no evidence of a separate Botswana "customs client code" distinct from the TIN** — the report should describe it as the BURS TIN rather than importing South African terminology.
- Sequencing for a first Indian shipment: company registration (CIPA) → BURS TIN → VAT registration → MTI import permit → appoint licensed clearing agent → (optionally) BURS deferment account with bank guarantee. The permit and the deferment account are the two lead-time items.

## Practical bottom line — % added to CIF value, by origin and product

### Takeaway
For every grade in scope (newsprint 4801.00; kraftliner 4804.11/4804.19; sack kraft 4804.21/4804.29; other kraft 4804.31–4804.59; testliner 4805.24/4805.25; fluting 4805.11/4805.12/4805.19), the customs-and-tax uplift over CIF is **14% from either origin** (duty 0% + VAT 14%), and **0% net** for a VAT-registered importer who recovers the input tax. The origins diverge on permits, paperwork and logistics, not on tax.

### Inferences
- The two "contrast" rows are the reason classification discipline matters: mis-shipping slit reels (≤36 cm, Chapter Note 8) or coated/graphic grades out of 48.04/48.05 turns a 0% duty into 5–15% and adds VAT on top, with the third-country consignment bearing it and the South African one not (SADC/intra-SACU columns are free even on those lines).
- If a SACU–India PTA is eventually concluded, it will make **no difference** to these paper grades, since the MFN rate is already free. Any report claim that a future PTA would improve Indian paper competitiveness in Botswana would be wrong for 48.01/48.04/48.05.

### Gaps
- **Freight, port, transit-bond, agent and demurrage costs are not quantified anywhere in this research.** The "+14%" figure is the customs-and-tax uplift only. The landed-cost comparison between India and South Africa turns almost entirely on those unquantified logistics items, and the report should say so rather than imply the origins are commercially equivalent.
- Botswana's own tariff file could not be inspected directly (not published online); all rates above rest on the SACU CET as published by SARS on 2026-08-28. Given that SACU members apply a common tariff by treaty, the risk of divergence is low but is not zero and is **not independently verified for Botswana**.
