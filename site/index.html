Why buyers use us

The part of a cross-border purchase nobody wants to own

Finding a factory is the easy half. The hard half is proving it is real, getting it to produce to your specification, moving the paperwork through two customs authorities and making sure the money lands safely. That is the part we take.

01

We verify before we quote

Establishment and licence numbers checked against the registers of the importing country, not taken from the seller's website. Lookalike exporter domains are common in this trade and we screen for them.

02

We price from customs data

We benchmark what comparable goods actually cleared customs at on your lane before we negotiate, so an offer can be judged against the market instead of against itself.

03

We take title

We are the buyer on one contract and the seller on the next. You deal with one counterparty, one invoice and one currency, in your own jurisdiction.

04

We mirror the terms

Inspection windows, weight tolerances, claim periods and bank charges are matched on both contracts, so nothing falls into the gap between supplier and buyer.

Process

From enquiry to delivered cargo

STEP 1

Specification

We turn a request into a buyable specification: grade, dimension or cut, pack, tolerance, standard and the certificates the destination country requires. Most failed imports fail here, before anyone has quoted.

STEP 2

Sourcing and verification

We approach producers directly, not through intermediaries, and check each one against the importing country's register. You receive at least three comparable offers on the same basis.

STEP 3

Landed price

Freight, insurance, port charges, duty and certification are added to the factory price so you see one number at your door, with the rate validity stated.

STEP 4

Contract and payment

We contract with the producer in our own name. Settlement by irrevocable letter of credit, documentary collection or staged transfer, with bank charges allocated in writing on both legs.

STEP 5

Production, inspection, shipment

Pre-shipment inspection where the value justifies it, photographs of the goods and pack before loading, and a full document set issued through our nominated forwarder.

Questions

What buyers and producers ask first

Do you only handle large volumes?

We work in full container loads, because that is where the economics hold. For a first order we normally run a trial of one or two containers on full commercial terms before a monthly programme starts.

Are you a broker or an agent?

Neither. We buy the goods in our own name and resell them. There is no commission on top of a producer's invoice, and the producer is not asked to manage a triangular billing arrangement.

Can you source a product you have never traded?

Yes, and most of our lanes started that way. What transfers between products is the method: specification, producer verification, landed costing, documents and settlement. What has to be built each time is the producer shortlist.

How do you protect the buyer against a factory that does not ship?

By never prepaying in full, by staging payment against production milestones and documents, and by checking the counterparty before any money moves. Where the value justifies it we add pre-shipment inspection by a third party.

Which Incoterms do you quote?

FOB, CFR, CIF, FCA and DAP. We prefer to quote at least two bases side by side so the buyer can see what the logistics portion actually costs.

We are a producer. Why work with you instead of selling direct?

Because we take the credit risk and the paperwork off your desk. You sell to one counterparty on terms you have agreed in advance, in one currency, and you are not asked to learn a new import regime.
